# Novel Revision — Second Edition Spec

Working document for the revision of *The Nine Lives of Ashcat* (novel/).
Source: four in-persona critical reviews (fantasy / mystery / young / older
reader), 2026-08-13. This file records (1) which findings were accepted,
modified, or rejected and why, and (2) the exact change list per chapter.
Rewrite agents execute section 3 verbatim; where the spec's description of
the current text is slightly off, honor the spec's *intent* against the
actual text.

---

## 1. Adjudication — what is fair, what does not fit this project

The project context that governs these calls: the novel is an adaptation of
the game and shares its canon (the four strengths/humours, the Hollow Court,
the Court of Whiskers, "spent is spent", "Moonlight" naming, the AI-honesty
stance). The novel may diverge from game *structure*, but not from canon
*facts*, and the owner's standing decisions (every choice needs a
consequence; repetition is the enemy) apply to the book as much as the game.

### ACCEPTED — fair, and fixable without breaking the book

1. **Dangling clues** (mystery critic's audit: 8 of 23 unpaid; fantasy critic
   concurs). The candle stub, the cart's cargo, the wrong-order lamps'
   purpose, the two prior stitchers, the Tatterstage motive, the Shear
   frame-job's purpose, the "elsewhere" mechanism, "Find the teacher."
   All get cashed. A mystery this scrupulous about its central clue cannot
   leave its founding exhibit uncalled.
2. **Deaths cost nothing the reader can feel** (three critics independently).
   The single highest-leverage fix in the file: the ch. 9 Toll takes a
   *specific* Remembered Day the reader has used, and Ash later reaches for
   it and finds it gone. "A quarter of everything banked, distantly" is,
   as the fantasy critic put it, an outrage in a book whose thesis is that
   spending must be felt. Also aligns with the game's own law: every choice
   needs a consequence.
3. **Ch. 10's law lecture** (all four, in different registers). Halve the
   lien exposition, hand the reader the key in plain words once, and convert
   the coincidence to causation: the Court summons Ash *because his file is
   attracting attention* (planted in ch. 9 already).
4. **One-temperature aphorism voice** (all four). De-aphorize two speakers
   (Merrow → clipped shift-boss fragments; Tansy → actual kitten syntax) and
   ration the narrator's "I want that in the record" tic to the prologue,
   the true deaths, the reveal, and the flats. The Clerk, Brindle, Cardew,
   and the Gentleman keep their registers — those are earning their keep.
5. **The victim drafts the climax** (mystery + older critic). Elspeth's note
   loses the verbatim disarming line; Ash composes it himself on the flats
   from what the box taught him. The note keeps the kettle absolution
   (trimmed per older critic: end at "three tries") — but the *argument*
   at the anchor must be the detective's own.
6. **No anger at Elspeth** (older critic; the deepest cut in the file, and
   correct). One scene at the end of ch. 11: the night Ash cannot forgive
   her. The forgiveness in ch. 12 will be worth double.
7. **Shear speaks the book's thesis one chapter early** (older critic).
   "It is not a technique. It is a life" comes out of her courtroom scene;
   she fails the stitch and withdraws in silence. The discovery returns to
   the shadow-stitching scene where it belongs.
8. **Bodkin is under-scened** (older critic; young critic adjacent). He
   appears alive twice before the flats and the ending asks those two scenes
   to carry forty years. He gets a third scene (ch. 10), a kindness Ash
   misreads.
9. **The Mere never negotiates** (fantasy + young critic). One offer, at the
   neap in ch. 9, in held voices. An antagonist in a novel of settlements
   gets one scene at the table. It offers what it actually holds (the
   missing); it cannot offer the dead — spent is spent, and the book's one
   iron law stays iron.
10. **The child's sunday jacket is never answered** (older critic). The
    epilogue answers it. And one coat stays on its peg — the epilogue of a
    book about loss keeps one visible loss.
11. **Line-level debris** (all): "like lit worms in a apple"; "flat as a
    ledger" twice; "patient as only thread is patient" twice; the unvaried
    death refrain's third use; "he had been listened to"; the
    goose/lattice/cathedral summary gag placed where tension needs to walk;
    the post-reveal recap that re-glosses every Bodkin scene at full length.
12. **The colors are a health bar in a trench coat** (young critic). The four
    strengths are game canon and stay, but the book explains them once,
    early, on their first use. (Canon: the pale strength is called
    Moonlight; the data name mysticism never appears in prose.)
13. **Timeline: "dead four times this season"** when the page has shown two
    (mystery critic). Reconcile the count against the actual on-page deaths.
14. **Trim the epilogue's bows** (fantasy critic) — compress, not gut; the
    window, the kettle, and "Come home" are untouchable.

### MODIFIED — right diagnosis, different medicine

15. **"Move the Bodkin reveal to ch. 9"** (young critic) vs. **"keep a
    suspect alive past ch. 7"** (mystery critic). The two asks conflict, and
    relocating the reveal re-plots a third of the book. Ruling: keep the
    reveal at ch. 11 and treat the *guessability* instead — the Court of
    Whiskers deputation gains a senior grey of the right vintage, planted in
    ch. 7 after the cat-shaped finding and eliminated honestly in ch. 11.
    The withholding chapters also get one beat where the refusal to write
    the name visibly costs Ash something, so the vigil reads as dread rather
    than stall.
16. **The caulker's shadow: expand it (older critic) vs. cut it (young
    critic).** The split means the parenthesis is the wrong size in both
    directions. Ruling: unbracket the resolution into the main text at
    roughly half its current total length, give the daughter one on-page
    beat, keep the wraith pointing at the Mere (it carries case-freight).
17. **"The Mere should take someone we love mid-book"** (young critic).
    Full version conflicts with the held-not-spent resolution mechanics.
    Ruling: the ch. 9 negotiation scene doubles as the on-screen taking —
    a named walk-in taken at the neap, returned with the rest in the
    epilogue. Menace on-screen, canon intact.
18. **"Put Tansy on the flats"** (young critic). Ash goes out alone; the
    Mere wants witnesses, not passengers, and the solitude is the scene.
    Ruling: Tansy gets the beat *before* the flats — she demands to come,
    and Ash gives her "Mind the house, Tansy," the book's own line handed
    down a generation. Pays the character AND the motif.
19. **Ch. 6 REFUSED comedy after the child's jacket** (older critic wants it
    cut/moved). The intake scenes carry the Clerk relationship, which every
    reader loved. Ruling: keep the scene, drop its temperature — after the
    jacket, this one refusal plays cold and frightening; the comedy resumes
    in later visits.

### REJECTED — does not fit the project

20. **Excise the Ratsmeet / Court of Whiskers as derivative** (fantasy
    critic). These are game canon and load-bearing (the Court minutes
    Tansy's case, standing, the deputation). The derivative-texture problem
    is real but the fix is the accepted voice work (items 4, 15), not
    excision. An adaptation does not amputate its own canon.
21. **Move the Fish-Giver to the epilogue** (fantasy critic). The Fish-Giver
    episode is the young and older readers' favorite part of ch. 4 and
    plants the epilogue's "eleven years, now twelve." It stays; ch. 4's fat
    is the rat-parliament length and the offstage magistrate's-cat favor
    chain (see change list).
22. **Soften/defend the AI-texture observation** — no action available or
    wanted; the disclosure stays per the owner's AI-honesty decision. The
    answer to "uniform polish" is item 4, executed hard.
23. **Cut the moth amicus / all ch. 10 humor** (young critic, partially).
    The moth stays — it is the chapter's best joke — but the Latin gets a
    plain-words key sentence, Snicket-style, and two of the four law-terms
    go.

## 2. What must not change (unanimous across all four reviews)

The kettle and its three tries; the mirror-stitch clue and the lap-side
reveal; ch. 7's blink reconstruction; the Clerk's cat-sized chair (never
mentioned by either party); the tea on the flats ("It was always both");
the window; "Come home"; every existing chapter-ending line; the prologue
essentially as-is.

## 3. Change list per chapter

Global (all agents, in their own files only):
- **G1.** Ration the record-keeping tic. Keep: prologue (up to two), each
  true death, the ch. 11 reveal, the flats. Delete or rephrase every other
  "I want that noted / in the record / entered in the record" variant.
- **G2.** Reduce "arithmetic" to at most twice per chapter.
- **G3.** One use each, book-wide, of "flat as a ledger" (keep ch. 2, cut
  ch. 8) and "patient as only thread is patient" (keep ch. 1, cut ch. 5).
- **G4.** Preserve every current chapter-ending line exactly.
- **G5.** LaTeX conventions: `---` em-dashes, ``` `` ``/`''` quotes,
  `\emph{}`, `\scenebreak`. No new packages, no structural macro changes.

### 00_prologue.tex

  establishes the device).

### 01_the_carrying.tex

- C1-2. G3: keep "patient as only thread is patient" here.

### 02_the_thread_itself.tex

### 05_audience_of_wax.tex

### 07_gargoyle_and_ghost.tex

### 09_the_quiet_roosts.tex
- C9-1. The Mere's one negotiation, at the neap: before or during the

  missing it holds in escrow) — the missing walk home *tomorrow* if the

  it at the book's best register, minimal jokes.
- C9-2. On-screen taking: a *named* walk-in — a lamplighter whose name the

  already cover him. Do not add an epilogue line; the existing "they walked

- C9-3. The Toll of the true death here takes a SPECIFIC Remembered Day:

  the Toll. Replace "a quarter of everything banked... I felt it go,

- C9-4. Death refrain ("The cold went. Then the noise...") — this third use

- C9-5. The fourth-suspect thread from C7-5 gets one sustaining beat (Ash

### 10_slow_clock_escrow_key.tex

## 4. Voice guardrails for all rewrite work

- Ash's narration: first person, dry, precise, fond; epigrams allowed but
  no longer mandatory — in the scenes marked emotional (C6-1, C9-1, C11-7,
  C12 all), prefer plain declarative sentences and let silence work.
- Metaphors must cash out to a concrete meaning (house style).
- No mechanics exposition in narrative voice; no future-telling; narration
  reports what happened.
- Referents resolve in the sentence they appear in.
- New prose must be indistinguishable in register from the book's best
  pages (the last Remembered Day, the tea on the flats, the window).

\chapter{The Quiet Gentleman}

Gravamen is where the city keeps everything it would rather not look at, which is why it is the best-looking district in Hollowmere.

The undertakers have money, and taste, and no neighbors who complain. Black brick, white trim, brass fittings with no fingerprints on them. The Horological Society keeps its tower there, running four minutes slow. The Undertakers' Circle keeps its quiet yards. And below it all, in a sense that is not quite geography, Gravamen is Court-adjacent: the Hollow Court's day-side face, the place where the paperwork of endings is drafted before it is filed in the building under everything. Debts drain to Gravamen the way water drains to the Mere. It is the city's low place for owing.

I did not arrive well. The arrangement I have with the reader is total honesty, and what happened on the way matters to the file.

The rag-wraiths took me at the boundary of the district, where the fog off the canals meets the crematory warmth and makes a weather with opinions. Three of them. I had never heard of two together, and here were three working as a \emph{set}, a wardrobe of the missing: a drover's coat, a housemaid's apron still tied at the waist of nobody, and a child's sunday jacket that I will not describe further, because some inventory belongs to no file.

The seam of the city was loosening. Cardew had said it, Merrow's lamps said it, and here it was said again in worsted and calico: more missing, more empty clothes walking, the Hush, which is the old binding that keeps the city's two sides politely strangers, fraying from the middle outward. The \emph{WATCH THIS} page would take the entry that night in three words, and I want them here too, in their ugly plainness: \emph{three, hunting together.}

I am going to set the fight down whole, because I paid for it, and this file does not waste receipts.

The apron came first, and I met it with the red. All of it at once, the parlor's lesson: spend, do not ration. One burst up its tied waist while it was still deciding to be a shape, and my teeth found the gather-knot at the back of it, where an apron keeps its whole grammar, and I unpicked it as I had learned on a wax keeper's lattice: the right loop, asked for and taken. The apron came apart into laundry. Laundry cannot fight. One.

The drover's coat was not going to be unpicked. Drover's stitching is tar-thread and rivet-seam, made for weather that hates you. So I gave it the green instead: patience, the goose's arithmetic, four seconds of looking bought at the price of standing still while a thing with no lungs came on. It swings from the shoulders, a drover's coat. It cannot help it; the shoulders are all it remembers. I let it overswing twice, read the third swing in the first two, and was not there for it. Then I put the black between us, the long watch, the soft foot, shadow off the crematory vents, and went around it the way night goes around a lamp.

Which left the child's sunday jacket, and the child's sunday jacket fought \emph{wrong}: small and quick and heedless, the way its owner must have been, all lunge and no keeping-anything-back, cuffs snapping like a game that has forgotten it is a game.

And here is the receipt I promised. I could not fight it properly. Everything I did to it was refusal and no answer, and the cold came off its empty collar in a way the pale strength had to meet. I spent her Moonlight on nothing but \emph{holding my own warmth on}. And while I was being merciful to a jacket, the drover's coat finished remembering where its shoulders were, and came down on me like weather off a hill.

Three is not one. I want that sentence in the file. I fought three of them well, and three is not one.

The cold went. Then the noise. Then the part of me that was worried about the noise.

The corridor. The filing. The desk. The Clerk was already shaking his head.

``You are not on the docket.'' He held the sheet up, translucent fingers, that winter-sounding pen laid down beside it. ``No appointment, no death. We do not process walk-ins.''

There was one chair in front of the desk. I sat in it. There had been three of them, I said. The child's jacket alone---

``A defeat off the docket spends no life,'' said the Clerk. ``You will be sent back up, minus a filing fee and whatever your satchel held. A \emph{true} death is announced. You will know the appointments when you keep them.''

He took the fee --- a measured pinch of my banked shine. Then he drew my thread out across the desk for the filing. Partway along it he stopped. There was a knot in the thread, small and grey, close-worked. He was still a moment too long. Then he took up the pen, wrote two words in the margin of my file, and laid it down again. I read the words upside down: CLERICAL ERROR.

He brought the stamp down. In all that quiet it was very loud.

The stamp said REFUSED. It was still a paw print.

``The gutter two streets east of where you fell drains poorly,'' he said, as I left, without looking up. ``If you are quick, the satchel will still be there. Your file is becoming irregular.''

The stairs up are longer than the fall down. They are no shorter for the practice.

\scenebreak

I retrieved the satchel. The Clerk's drainage intelligence was, like everything at that desk, exact.

Then I went back and read the ground, because a cat who has been killed by something owes himself four seconds of looking at where it happened, and because the entry I had made --- \emph{three, hunting together} --- was the wrong entry. It was the entry a frightened animal makes. Here is the right one.

Rag-wraiths drift. That is the whole of their nature: they are clothes with the person gone out of them, and they go where the weather goes. Two of them in one street is what two leaves in one gutter is, which is nothing. Drift leaves a track. Wet fog lies down behind a drifting thing in a long smeared wake, and I have followed such wakes for sport since I was small enough to be silly.

There were no wakes. There were three dry patches.

Three, on the wet, at the corner where the canal fog meets the crematory warmth: three shapes' worth of stone that the night's damp had not reached, because something had been standing on that stone long enough to keep it dry. Dust had banked against the hems. They had not blown into that corner. They had been \emph{waiting} in it, in position, since before the fog came up, and they had been waiting in a corner no man crosses, on the low road, the gutter-and-ledge line a cat takes into Gravamen when he does not want to use the bridge.

I sat down in the middle of the three dry patches and made myself finish the sum.

Somebody had known which crossing I would use. Not which district --- the district was on a counterfoil, and a counterfoil is a thing that can be guessed --- but the \emph{crossing}, the cat's crossing, chosen by a party who understood that I would come in low and unbridged and alone at the wrong end of the night.

And under the drover's coat's collar, where a coat is gathered and where nobody looks because nobody expects a coat to have been gathered by anyone, there was a knot. Small. Grey. Tied with a little finishing twist at the top of it that I had last seen going backward down a warehouse wall on the Mere-edge, an evening and a district ago.

I did not know whose hand. I knew it was a hand. Rag-wraiths do not tie themselves to a corner and hold their positions for a cat, any more than three coats independently decide to hunt as a set. Something had gathered them, and posted them, and paid whatever posting costs, and the thing it had bought with that money was the specific street I was going to walk.

I have written that this file does not waste receipts. Here is the one I wanted least: from that corner onward the case was no longer a thing I was investigating. It was a thing that was also investigating me, and it had gone first.

I climbed to a Gravamen roof in the last of the night to put my coat and my nerve back in order, and that is when I saw the third thread.

Understand what the sky of this city looks like to me by now. Threads everywhere, always: the day-side's ten thousand small tethers, and over them the great slow cables of the guilds, and here and there, rare as comets, a \emph{familiar-thread} --- the deep silver line that runs between a witch and her bound creature, the kind I wore for fifteen years, the kind that hangs cut at my chest with one strand left. You cannot mistake one for anything else. It has a note in it. It hums with the two lives it joins, and the hum is a chord.

Over Gravamen, strung high between the Horologists' slow tower and the fog, ran a familiar-thread humming a chord of one.

Not mine. Mine hangs at my own chest, and I know its weight without looking. Not hers. Hers I would know under floodwater, and hers ended, cut clean, over the black glass of the Mere. A third: old silver gone the grey of long weather, taut, tended, \emph{alive}, and with no witch on the end of it. I sat very still on that roof and followed it with my eyes as far as eyes would go: south and west, out over the terraces, toward the water. A familiar-thread with one voice in the chord. A bound creature whose witch does not answer, and whose thread has not been cut.

The ledger in my chest opened to the page with the two nines on it, and I did not write the line between them, and my not-writing it had begun to feel like holding a door shut with my back.

There was a knot in the thread's far slack, I thought. Too far to be sure: a small worked interruption in the line, the size of nothing. And while I stared at it, dawn came up over Gravamen, and somewhere below me a door I had not noticed opened, and stayed open, in the manner of a door that is \emph{for} you.

I had come to find the Quiet Gentleman. I should have known better than to think finding him was a thing I would do, rather than a thing that would be done to me.

\scenebreak

His office is not hidden. That is the first thing to report about it: no cellar, no password, no crooked stair. A tall black-brick house on Gravamen's best square, brass plate polished blank, blank like the card, door ajar at the exact angle of expectation. Inside, a hall smelling of fog and beeswax. \emph{Beeswax}; item. And beyond it a long room of shelves that I mistook, for one honest heartbeat, for the Hollow Court's, because they were filled floor to ceiling with folders tied in ribbon.

Not grey ribbon. Every color. Every color a promise is made in.

He was at the window, looking out at the square, and I want to do for the reader what nobody managed to do for me, which is describe him. I cannot. Neither can anyone else; I have since asked rats, magpies, gargoyles, and a ghost. He is a gentleman, and he is quiet. There is a coat, of good cloth; there are gloves. Above the collar the eye slides, politely, the way it slides off other people's grief.

Nobody believes that is his name. The old ones on the ropewalk roof gave me the rule with the rest of their lore, flatly, in the tone they keep for things that are not up for discussion: you do not say an account-holder's true name, because saying it is how an account with him is opened. So the city has called him something else for so long that something else is now the only thing there is to call him.

No one remembers meeting him for the first time. And I cannot tell you whether that morning was my first.

The room was strange to me and my paws knew the floor.

``The witch's cat.'' His voice is the sound a signature would make. ``Correction. The Needle's \emph{heir}. The estate is open, the heir is in the room; the description is amended to the later instrument.''

He turned from the window.

``Acquisitions to date. A magpie's regard. A lamplighter's knot. An old mother's debt. A guildmaster, ruined, converted to gratitude. One purr, recovered against paper of this house.'' Nothing in his tone accused. Accusation is settlement's first cousin, and settlement is the one thing that starves him. ``The collection is competent, little rival. That is a professional finding. It is not a kindness. Kindness is a term, and no terms are agreed between us.''

``I have questions.''

``You have \emph{payment}.'' It was not a correction so much as a page turned to the correct one. ``Questions are held by nobody. Payment is held by you. The whole of this house stands in that distinction.'' He gestured, and I understood the gesture was the room's whole law. On a reading-table by the window lay a single folder, untied, and beside it, in a dish, exactly the space where a favor-knot would sit.

What I did next I set down in the way my witch would pass on review, so: I looked first. Four seconds and more. The folder was the counterfoil's twin, his own file of the transaction, the quiet channel's other end. The price in the dish's emptiness was one knot, unspecified, \emph{his choosing later}. That is his whole game in one dish: not the knot you give him, but the not-knowing which. I thought of Tansy's contract, of the old ones on the ropewalk roof: \emph{he never signs anything that can be settled; read it before you touch it.} And I thought: a cat has one thing a gentleman structurally cannot have, which is the option of walking away from a table with something already on it.

So I paid him nothing, and I asked my questions anyway, and here is the strangest entry in this whole strange file: \emph{he answered them}. Freely. Precisely. I have come to believe the folder on the table was never the offer; the offer was the conversation, and the conversation was bait of another kind. The Gentleman does nothing that is not collection.

What he told me, with the fog pressing its face to his good windows:

That yes: the night's arrangements had come through his house. Three favors, called blind, brokered without asking. ``The not-asking is the price of the quiet. The Guildmaster states it correctly. It is stated correctly in most rooms this house has papered, and it is never stated in writing.'' Item: a cart-slot from the Chandlers. Item: a scattering of seed by a human hand. ``The hand was human. The debt was not.'' I sat with that sentence while the clock in his hall ticked four minutes behind the world, and it opened like a lattice: some day-sider, indebted to a night-side creditor, worked off the debt with an evening's honest labor, hauling seed to a roofline. The debtor human; the debt's \emph{owner} something else.

``And the third favor?'' I asked.

``The third favor,'' said the Gentleman, ``emptied a hall.''

The room was very quiet. Even his clock seemed to owe its ticks somewhere.

``I do not know where the Lamplighters went,'' he said, and I believed him --- I, who read rooms for a living, believed him, and I was right to, and being right about him was somehow worse. ``The instrument is a debt of the hall's. Old oil, older gratitude. The form of payment is: \emph{be elsewhere, all of you, from seven bells}. Where elsewhere is, is not a term of the paper. The purposes of clients are not recited in the paper. I am a channel, cat. Water does not ask.''

``Water,'' I said, ``is what the drowned say too.''

Something happened then that I have no language for: the place where his face is \emph{considered} me. ``Yes,'' he said, at length, approvingly, as if I had correctly totaled a column. ``You will want to know about my client.''

I had not asked. Asking would have been payment --- eagerness is a coin; he mints it. He told me regardless, and every sentence had the weight of a thing given for a reason I could not see. That is the only way he gives anything:

``This house meets everyone. This house met you before you were worth meeting. This client is unmet.'' Not \emph{I have never met}. \emph{Unmet}: a condition of the file, with no tense in it anywhere, the way a clause has no tense. ``Instructions received in the manner instructions are sometimes received in this trade. A knot, tied overnight, in the binding-thread of my own ledger, inside my own house, the commission written in the tying. Close work. Old-fashioned. No hand of that description has been on my paper since this was a much smaller concern.''

The Clerk's voice, unbidden, from a refusal I had not yet had and a night I had already had: \emph{whoever ties these does not care to be noticed.} Two desks, both in the debt trade, one knot-hand neither could file.

``And the payment?'' I asked. ``What does a client with no face pay a house like this?''

The Gentleman crossed to his shelves, drew out a shallow drawer that had no business fitting between two others, and set its contents on the reading-table beside the untouched folder: a spool. Small, plain, wound with thread of a grey I knew and my whole body did not want to know: the grey of the shroud-seam, the grey of tide-taken things. Wound \emph{tight}. Years and years of it.

``Time,'' he said. ``Stored. Spent-down life, wound and banked. It is not a currency I deal in twice; it does not settle, it only \emph{ends}, and ending is against the interests of my house. But a channel takes the toll the water carries.''

He looked at the spool the way Wick had looked at the thread on his blotter. ``The number of beings in Hollowmere competent to wind such a spool is four. Inquiries have been made. Three of the four are dead. One of the three is your witch. The winding is not her hand.'' A pause with no apology in it anywhere. ``It is her hand's \emph{mirror}.''

He set the spool a half-inch further from the folder, as if the two accounts should not touch. ``Price the stock, little rival. The house priced it when it crossed the table; the working is available to you. Nothing living winds its own life and survives the winding. What is on that spool is not saved. It is taken, and taken small. A gargoyle's blink. A street's held breath. The quiet between a bell and its echo. Half-seconds nobody enters as missing, gathered over decades, wound tight, banked. That is the stock.

``Under the stock, an advance. Something in this city holds lives in escrow. It has been lending against its holdings. Whether it is entitled to lend is not a question this house is instructed to reach. My client did not ask. Water does not ask.''

\scenebreak

I walked home the long way, over the roofs of three districts, because there was too much in the satchel of my chest and walking is how a cat files.

Entered into the case, that morning, in order:

That the murder night was purchased entire: silence, transport, and an emptied hall, all of it bought through the city's one channel that never asks, by a client even the channel has not seen.

That the client pays in wound-down life: years off a spool, the coin of the Toll itself, the one currency in Hollowmere that cannot be earned honestly by anything that means to go on living. The Gentleman's phrase, in my ear the whole way home, keeping time with my feet: \emph{it does not settle, it only ends.}

That the client's knot-work is close, old-fashioned, mirror-handed. At the Gentleman's ledger. At my own thread under the Clerk's lamp. At a warehouse wall on the Mere-edge. And at the collar of a drover's coat, on a corner of my own route, holding three empty garments in position against the hour I would come. One hand, everywhere, and every place it touched, a professional shrugging: \emph{not one of ours.}

That the last of those is a different kind of entry from the others, and I made myself write it in the same hand as the rest, which took longer than it should have. The first four are a hand at work on the city. The fifth is a hand at work on \emph{me}. I have been running this file as though the far end of it were sitting still and waiting to be arrived at. The far end knows my crossings.

That over Gravamen hums a familiar-thread with one voice in its chord, running toward the water, and I had not followed it, and I told myself it was because the case comes before the curiosities, and the ledger of my chest recorded instead, in the small honest hand it keeps for entries against myself, that I had been \emph{afraid} of where it went.

And that the Quiet Gentleman was now, formally, a suspect: the second strong name on a board that had just lost Wick. He knew it. He found it fitting. He had all but pinned himself to my board with his own gloved hands.

I put the corner to him too, walking home, and could not make it sit. A house that brokers does not stand three coats on a kerb; it sells the standing to somebody who will. So the posting went through some channel, and the channel is a business, and a business keeps paper. Somewhere in this city there is a receipt for my death, made out four hours before I walked into it, and I did not have the price of asking him for it.

Guilty of brokering, unasking, of profiting on the night my witch died: certainly. Guilty of the needle-work itself? I did not know. That is the entry, whole, in the small honest hand: \emph{I do not know what he is capable of, because I do not know what he is}, and not-knowing, about a creature who papers rooms you have slept in, is its own species of cold. He stays. He stays at the top of the board until the method itself rules him out, and nothing less will do it.

Tansy was awake when I came in, upside down against the coal scuttle, supervising me pin the morning to the board. ``What is \emph{escrow},'' she said. Not a question mark in it; she bills her questions flat, like a tradeswoman.

Escrow, I told her, is when somebody else holds a thing of yours, so that neither of you can touch it until the argument about it is over.

She thought about this with her whole body, the way she does. ``Like when the big kittens hold your tail.''

Exactly like that, I said, and she went to sleep mid-purr, and I sat up over the board a long time with her one word ringing in me like a struck bowl. \emph{Holds.} Something in this city holds lives in escrow. Held is an argument that is not over. Held is not spent.

The method. Everything now ran through the method: who in this city can spend life like money and stitch like her mirror. I needed to date the technique --- how long had that hand been working? --- and for dating old crimes, Hollowmere keeps exactly two archives that never lie. One is under everything, and charges a toll.

The other has been standing on a corner of Wickrow for three hundred years, and it had, I had heard from the pigeons, recently done something unprecedented.

It had blinked.

\chapter{The Remembered Days}

Before the dreams, the living. Two more of them enter the file here, and one of the two is a suspect.

The Witches' Circuit convened at the quarter-moon, in the long room above the Assay Court that they have used since before the Assay Court existed, and Mother Cardew brought me in under her cloak-hem, which is to say: she opened the door, looked at me sitting in the corridor, said ``the cat is with me,'' and dared eight empty chairs and seven occupied ones to comment.

Nine seats. One empty in the way that seats are empty when someone has died --- draped, honored, unspeakable. And one empty in the way that seats are empty when someone is \emph{late}, which in that room is its own scandal.

The Circuit maintains the Hush. That is the whole of their charter: nine working witches, each holding a district's worth of seams, together holding the great old binding that keeps Hollowmere's two sides politely separate. They are paid like plumbers and respected like surgeons and they frightened me, all seven, the way competence frightens: these were hands that could have tied me off like a loose thread. And every one of them, I saw within a minute, was more frightened than I was. The Needle was dead. The Needle's seams --- her whole district's worth, and the other thing, the great thing nobody at that table would name with a cat present --- wanted a new Needle, and the seven of them together, Cardew said later, without bitterness, as a surgeon reports a margin: \emph{could not sew one year of her decade}.

The late seat arrived. Mistress Shear.

I will describe her fairly, because the file demands it and because I came, in the end, almost to pity her. Sharp-boned, quick, dressed a half-degree finer than the room; a candidate's hunger worn thin over real skill --- she is not a fraud; I watched her hands the whole session and the hands are good, journeyman-good, lattice-good. She had petitioned for the ninth seat twice while Elspeth lived and been declined twice, because the ninth seat is the Needle's seat and she does not have the hand, and everyone at the table knew it, and she knew they knew, which is the specific acid that etches ambition into something worse.

She spoke correctly of the dead. She moved correctly through the agenda. And when the session closed she stayed behind the others to examine, with correct and scholarly interest, the sampler of Elspeth's stitching that the Circuit keeps under glass.

I watched her want it. Her hands came up over the case and stopped --- a half-inch off the glass, spread, held --- the way a paw hangs over water with a fish in it: everything committed, nothing yet spent. She stood like that longer than she knew, her face doing the correctness and her hands doing the truth, and in the glass under her fingers, reflected, the draped ninth chair. Then the hands came down to the frame's edge, and she examined the sampler \emph{mirror-wise}, tracing the lines right to left, and my whole pelt stood up, quietly, under the draped chair where I had failed to leave with Cardew.

I took that to Brindle, because appetite for technique leaves receipts, and the Exchange reads receipts.

It took the Magpie three dawns and cost me the redeeming of the thimble --- which I had always meant to redeem; the transaction, for what it is worth, was a pleasure on both sides of the counter. What Brindle's paper-net brought up was this: some weeks before the murder, a circular had moved through the quiet channels --- \emph{the} quiet channels; fog-smelling paper; no signature but a small inked knot --- offering for sale, to a discreet and qualified buyer, ``the Needle's hand, demonstrated and taught.'' Stolen technique. The stitch of Elspeth Vane, on the market, while Elspeth Vane was alive.

And Mistress Shear had answered it. Paid earnest-money --- heavily; there were promissory notes in the Exchange's tributary streams with her careful signature on them --- and received, by way of demonstration, a sample: a hand's length of warding, unpicked in the Vane stitch, mirror-handed, delivered to her workroom inside a locked box she swears was never unlocked.

Then: nothing. No teaching, no second contact, her notes uncollected, her earnest-money never drawn. The seller had dangled the one thing her hunger could not refuse, proved it real, taken her name --- and vanished, leaving her holding a sample of the exact technique that would shortly unpick a murder scene.

You see what that made her. Suspect the third: a witch with the motive of an empty seat, the ambition of two declined petitions, and \emph{physical possession of the murder method}, bought on paper that smelled of fog. And you see --- but I did not, not yet; in the order I lived it she went on the board beside the Gentleman with a red thread of her own --- what it made the seller. Somebody had spent effort, before the murder, to hang the method around a hungry woman's neck. The fair reading and the foul reading of Mistress Shear arrived together and sat on my board refusing to share the pin.

\scenebreak

The second entry came up from the Mereside, as everything in this half of the book comes up from the Mereside, with wet footprints that were not wet.

A caulker on the drowned terraces had sold his shadow years ago --- men do, down there; the pawnshops of the impossible advance real coin on one --- and years on, the shadow came home. It hung itself correctly off his heels and set about his life, and it was \emph{better at his life than he was}: it paid the arrears his shame had let stand, watered the fern, answered his estranged daughter's letters in a hand like his hand mended. She was coming to visit, the first time in six years, and the man wanted the shadow gone. I took the case because I am a creature with a nine-pocketed shadow sewn by a dead woman's hand; the file was always going to find me.

Two nights of shadowing a shadow settled it. The shadow was not his. It was a rag-wraith --- but one that wore not clothes but a \emph{silhouette}, the whole outline of a man, and it was not attacking anyone. It was rehearsing. A man's evenings, a man's kindnesses, a man's handwriting --- practicing being human, carefully, getting it right --- because its own man is one of the missing, and it had apprenticed itself to the nearest empty life that matched its shape.

I sat on the caulker's wall at three in the morning with the borrowed shadow beside me, and I asked it where its own man had gone. It did not speak; wraiths do not. It \emph{pointed} --- the first I have ever heard of one pointing --- one long silhouette-arm, unhurried, certain, out past the sea-wall, at the black glass of the water.

The missing drain toward the Mere. The wax feeds toward the Mere. Her thread was cut over the Mere. The one-voiced familiar-thread hums toward the Mere. I thanked the shadow, formally. It bowed the way its man must once have bowed, and went back to the fern.

I told the Court of Whiskers what the shadow was; the Court told the pawnshop; and night-side law, which is older than mercy but not unrelated to it, ruled the man's pledge lapsed and his own true shadow returnable on payment of interest. The interest was: the wraith keeps its evenings at the fern. I was on the wall when the daughter came. The door opened before her knock --- the man himself, flat-lit, trembling, unassisted --- and I watched her face do the arithmetic: the answered letters against the shaking hand, the mended writing against the man in the doorway, six years against one step over the sill. She went in. The wraith sits with them some evenings, in the correct chair, rehearsing. Nobody minds.

\scenebreak

And then I went hunting the past deliberately, and the way you hunt the past is: you sleep where it is not safe to sleep.

I had the shape of the thing by then. The Remembered Days come on the pale strength; they are Moonlight spending itself as memory when it cannot spend as anything else, and they come strongest where the body lies down inside old danger --- some economy in the unfinished magic she left me that pays out her past against my risk, a purse that opens only when the paw trembles. Sleep on the hearthrug and you dream of dinner. Sleep on the Wickhouse rafters over the water wall, over the fed thing turning below, and---

\emph{Warm. Lamplight. The table. The chart of the water wall, half-sewn, and me small and certain on the middle of it, and her voice above, amused, talking to the needle: ``That winter I tried eight strays\ldots''}

I have given the reader that one already. But dreams, revisited, keep drafts, like the mastiff's street: this time it ran longer. This time her needle stopped, in the dream, and her hand came down and rested on the small sleeping certainty of me, and she said --- to the cat on the chart, to the room, to the accounts she was forever keeping in the margins of herself --- ``Nine is the correct number. Nine is the number I owe.'' And the needle went back to the wall-chart, and the word \emph{owe} went into me like a splinter too fine to find again once it is in.

I slept on the sea-wall at the Mereside edge, in the fog's own doorway, with Sootbeard --- who had insisted; the operational posture of the watch, he said, extends to colleagues sleeping irregularly --- standing over me like a monument to bad decisions. And I dreamed the forty-year night.

I will report it as it came, in pieces, because it came in pieces and I do not trust myself to smooth it.

\emph{Nine bells.} That is the first piece and it rang through all the others: the tower giving its full old count, one to nine, and the ninth bell's voice was deep and certain and it is the only time I have ever heard it, awake or asleep, and I knew it instantly the way you know a name called across a market: the city used to have one more hour of night, and the hour had a bell, and the bell was rung that evening on purpose, because the working needed the whole nine.

\emph{The barge.} Black water lying dead calm and lightless, and out on it, a barge without a bargeman, and the Circuit of forty years ago on the quays with every lamp in the city turned toward the Mere --- and the lamps' order mattering; the Lamplighters walking their sequence like a loom's shuttle, lighting a pattern across the whole town, the entire city made into a stitching-frame; I woke with my paws working and slept back down into it.

\emph{Her.} Young --- younger than I ever knew her, hair dark, hands already hers --- going out onto the black glass \emph{alone}, on foot, on the water, which held her the way paper holds ink, because the working wanted the Needle and no other weight. And the fog on the far shore stood up in shapes that were not the fog, and under the water something \emph{hoped} --- I felt it hope, in the dream, the way you feel thunder through the pads --- something old and patient and vast, risen the whole way to the underside of the surface, pressed against the world like a face against glass. The Mere, before the seam. What the city was, that season. What all the lamps were for.

\emph{The grey shape.} At the edge of every piece of the dream, close to her skirts, on the black water where nothing else could walk except her and it: a smallness. Grey. Four-footed. The dream kept it at the corner, always at the corner --- dreams are tactful; hers especially; even remembered, she was minding what a cat should see --- but I know how a familiar stands to a witch at work. I have stood so for fifteen years. The grey shape stood so.

\emph{The seam.} And then the working itself, which I cannot render and will not try beyond this: she sewed the lake shut. Standing on it. Stitch by stitch across a closing that ran from the Wickhouse shore to the far fog, her needle taking the light of the ordered lamps as thread takes wax, the ninth bell tolling the measure --- and each great stitch had to be \emph{paid at the pull}, the way all her work was paid, costs visible, nothing free; and I watched the payments go: memory --- a whole quarter of the sky of the dream went dark and stayed dark; warmth --- the winter that followed was in her hands for the rest of her life; luck; years.

And then the working reached the last stitches, the anchor of the whole seam, and the price of an anchor is not memory or warmth or luck or years, and I saw --- at the corner, at the tactful terrible corner of the dream --- eight small lights go out of the grey shape at her skirts, one at a time, each one drawn down her needle into the stitching like beads onto a string, eight lives pulled out of a living familiar and sewn into the bottom of the lake, and the grey shape did not run, and that is the detail I woke on, both times I have dreamed it since: \emph{it did not run}. It stood to her, the way a familiar stands, while it was spent.

She came back off the water alone.

The dream would not show me the shape's face. I have said dreams are tactful. But it showed me her ledger --- the one she kept in the margins of herself, where I had heard her write the word \emph{owe} --- and the entry the working made there, and the entry was: \emph{eight}. And fifteen years ago, on a chart-table, to a sleeping stray, the same voice said: \emph{nine is the number I owe.}

Eight spent. Nine owed. She was not keeping count of strays for whimsy. She was doing what she did with every debt in her shambles of accounts: rounding \emph{up}, against interest, and paying forward the only way cat-magic pays.

\scenebreak

I woke on the sea-wall in the grey before dawn with Sootbeard's granite watching over me and the fog going out on the ebb, and I lay still a long time, doing what I had been refusing to do since a roof in Gravamen, which was: drawing the line between the nines.

Forty years ago, my witch sewed the Mere shut and paid the anchor of the seam with eight lives that were not hers. Her familiar of that era --- grey, spent, unpaid --- went missing from every record I have ever heard recited, and \emph{missing-not-dead} is the category this entire case has been orbiting like a moth at a shutter: the coats, the wraiths, the Court's returned filings. The technique that killed her is forty years old, works in her mirrored hand, and spends stored life as coin --- and the only account in the city that ever held stored life in that quantity was emptied into the bottom of the lake, all but one.

And the thing under the water, which hoped, has been \emph{finding} things ever since the seam began to loosen: finding wax, finding the missing, finding --- the world-bible of my own pelt told me, there on the cold stone --- someone. It has found someone. Someone with forty years of grievance and one life left and her exact stitch learned from in front, mirror-wise, across a decade of lamplit evenings on the near side of the work.

I did not write the name. Even in the chest-ledger, even in the small hand. Some entries, once made, cannot be unpicked, and I am my witch's heir in this above everything: I do not sew what I am not yet prepared to pay for.

But I stood up on the sea-wall, and I looked out at the flat black glass, and then up, at the one-voiced familiar-thread humming its long grey line over the city, toward the water, toward the fog that holds its shapes too long.

The pigeons of the Mereside roosts, I noticed, were silent. Still. Again. The quiet roosts: the silence had never really lifted down here, this close to the water --- it had been \emph{bought} here first, and bought deepest.

Whatever I believed and was refusing to believe, the next thing was the same either way. Follow the thread. All the way. To whatever is on the end of it.

I went home first, and slept the honest kind of sleep, and ate the breakfast Tansy had saved me half of --- she saves me half; I have given up litigating it --- and I sharpened every claw I own on the doorpost of a dead woman's house, and then I went to spend some lives.

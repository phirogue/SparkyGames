# Story bible 4: timeline, and who knows what when

Any scene that contradicts this is wrong and gets changed. Any character who
acts on knowledge they do not have on this chart is wrong and gets changed.

---

## Deep past

- Hollowmere is built on the Mere because the Mere is the wealth: deeper water
  than any coast for a hundred miles, ships up the fog, quays rich before they
  were safe.
- The water collects. The city pays a tithe: **the first of everything** —
  first catch of a season, first light of a new hearth, first child of a bad
  year. Nobody calls it monstrous. Everybody's grandmother paid it, and
  everybody's grandmother ate that winter.
- Nine bells. The ninth rung slow on mornings after the water had collected,
  one stroke for each thing taken.

## ~100 years ago

- A plague season. Tolls go unpaid. The city, in council, under seal, **pawns
  the ninth bell to the Hollow Court** as collateral. Never redeemed; the
  docket stays open.
- Since that night the city's nights run one hour short. Nobody in Hollowmere
  has dreamed a dream to the end in a century, and nobody finds it strange.

## Year 0 — forty years before the book

**The city is one tide from a bill it has owed since the first quay.**

1. The Circuit assembles. The ninth bell is signed out of the vault for one
   night, on the Circuit's surety, "for the great sewing."
2. The Lamplighters walk their sequence as a pattern across the whole town: the
   city made into a stitching-frame.
3. Elspeth Vane — young, dark-haired, the only needle strong enough — walks out
   onto the black water alone, because the working wants the Needle and no
   other weight.
4. The seam is sewn, paid at the pull: memory, warmth, luck, years.
5. **The anchor asks one finished life.** Hers is mortgaged to the seam while
   she lives. There is none to tender. **Bodkin — nine years her familiar —
   offers.** He says it to the clerk of the working, not to her, so that it
   will stand in the record where she cannot unsay it: *He offers. Write down
   that he offers.*
6. Eight lives drawn down her needle, one at a time. He stands to her and does
   not run. Docket left open as to the balance.
7. She comes back off the water alone. He walks into the fog on the one life
   she left him.

**Same season:** the Horologists' founder sets the tower's crown-wheel; the
city owes him one sunrise, dated on completion. Never paid.

**The week after the water:** Elspeth writes her confession against herself
while the hand still shakes, deposits it as **Box 9-Crown** with the
Locksmiths' Fellowship (condition: *the death of the depositor, plus one
season*; claimant: *my heir, in law*), and pays the box-fee in tears —
escrow-grade, one vial, brokered by the Quiet Gentleman, who has never sold it.
The same week she buys **one beeswax candle, escrow-grade**, and keeps it
forty years unburned.

**Also Year 0, late:** the Tatterstage's leading lady — secretly a stitcher,
seam under the greasepaint — dies mid-performance, her throat closed by bought
fog. The Understudy goes on in her place and dies mid-line the same night, of a
silence that had been purchased. **Bodkin's first killing: he is removing the
hands that could re-anchor the seam.** Cardew signs the closing.

## Year 3

**Widow Sarn of Hemlock Row** — sailmaker, stitcher, taught half the quay to
mend nets — found in her own kitchen. Her terrier sits two winters on the step.
Cardew signs the closing. Nobody looks, either time.

## Year 10

Elspeth mails a parcel to her future self: her first spool, ordinary grey linen
thread, no working in it. She opens it again some years later, adds a note
sealed with a geranium petal — *For after. You will know when after is* — and
reseals it.

## Years 3–25

Elspeth tries, over the years, **eight times** to make a new bond. None of the
eight stays long enough for the working to be completed. (Only the ninth
receives the nine lives — this is why the Clerk has not filed a nine in
centuries.)

## Year 25 — fifteen years before the book

**Ash.** A stray who stays. She completes the working: nine lives, on her own
needle, out of her own evenings. She names him.

He never works a case, never holds an office, never is asked for anything. He
is kept, warm, at the correct times, for fifteen years, and assumes it is
because he is loved. It is. It is also restitution.

Bodkin sits on the sea-wall and watches the lamp burn.

## Year 29 — eleven years before

Pell begins putting scraps out at dawn. The Horologists begin skimming minutes.

## Year 30 — ten years before

The Mereside roosts begin emptying, bird by bird, going down to the water to be
paid.

## The spring of the book

- Elspeth orders **forty spools of deep silver** and writes to the Circuit for
  every steady needle it can spare, "for a work of renewal, before winter, that
  I have put off too long and will not put off past this year." **She means to
  make the seam permanent.**
- **This is why now.** While the seam can be opened, Bodkin's debt stays
  collectible. A permanent seam prices his waiting at nothing, forever.
- **Wick** buys every debt Elspeth owes, in one week, and calls them due on
  **Lady's Day**.
- A circular moves through the quiet channels — fog-smelling paper, no
  signature but an inked knot — offering "the Needle's hand, demonstrated and
  taught." **Shear** answers, pays earnest-money, receives a mirror-handed
  sample in a locked box, then nothing. (Bodkin building a frame that will keep
  the Circuit watching the living.)
- Days before the murder: good seed hauled **up** to the rooftops by hand and
  scattered wide. The Post is bought quiet **in advance**.
- Wisps are taught the lamplighters' route, night after night, until appetite
  keeps a schedule.

## The murder night — hour by hour

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Morning | Elspeth tidies the workroom. Sets the second-best thimble square. Washes the red kerchief and folds it on the sewing table where a cat coming over the sill cannot miss it. Carries Ash off her work to the hearthrug: *"Mind the house, Ash. I am going to see about the lamps."* |
| Day | She goes to the Lamplighters' hall. Her silver thread snags on a lamp-hook. The hall is emptied — every soul in it told there is work elsewhere, by the one voice that knew where elsewhere was. |
| Dusk | Lamps go up. Eight bells. **She closes her shutters, which she has never done.** Sets out the beeswax candle, unlit. Puts the kettle on. For two. |
| Dusk | Ash takes the vole on the Fish-Giver's roof. Sees a silver thread that is nobody's he knows. Declines to follow it. |
| | A cart, unlit, wheels wrapped, driven by a day-sider whose debt Bodkin held and called: down the lane riding light, back toward the Mere riding heavy — *street-thread, harvested, spooled*. The Quiet Gentleman walks behind it at strolling pace, attending his own blind commission. |
| 7 bells + 4 min | Bodkin crosses Old Counting Row, buying Sootbeard's blink with a stolen half-second. |
| | He walks Needle Lane's lamps **in reverse order**. A round run backward is an unpicking: her ward is off the lane before he reaches the gate. |
| | **She lets him in.** *"You'll take tea, then."* He lights her candle. They sit an hour. He does not spend four seconds looking, because if it was welcome he could not have collected. |
| Between the boiling and the scream | The work is done. Faster than any needle honestly goes. He pinches out the candle. |
| Meanwhile | Ash fights a wisp, notices no pigeons, reads the lamps lit wrong, meets the wraith on the lane and loses, and **takes the long way home** — eleven minutes — because the route had a wrongness at the head of it. |
| | He finds the hall open and empty, its kettle cold, her thread on the hook. Runs eleven streets. Kettle screaming. The shape in the parlor doorway, wearing her walls. He goes for it and dies. |
| | **Bodkin posts Ash's death as an appointment** — the gentlest slot on the ledger. The Court's own posting line stays blank. |
| Night | Ash wakes. Takes the kettle off the fire: three tries. Puts his head through the kerchief. Finds one uncut strand running out from under the window. |

### Who knows what, that night

| | Knows |
|---|---|
| **Bodkin** | Everything. |
| **The Gentleman** | That he walked behind a cart of harvested thread. Blind for the client is not blind for the house. |
| **The Mere** | It commissioned this. |
| **Cardew** | Felt the ninth seat go out at dusk — the load of the Hush came onto seven pairs of hands. Knows a Needle has died. Does not know how, and will not walk Needle Lane in the dark while something is unpicking it. **This is why the witches come at first light.** |
| **Ash** | Nothing. |
| **Wick** | Nothing. Her death destroys his plan. |
| **Shear** | That she bought a stolen technique from a seller who vanished. |

## The season of the case — what Ash knows, chapter by chapter

| Ch | Ash's state of knowledge |
|---|---|
| 1 | It has happened twice before, both stitchers. He was "her ninth." |
| 2 | The killer reads the Weft, travels the overstreets, and cuts clean without the right to. He notes *it travels like a cat* and turns the page. |
| 3 | Bodkin exists, has a name, and gave him a rule. The name means nothing to him. Wick is buying her debts. |
| 4 | The city once paid a tithe. There was a ninth bell. Paper has teeth. |
| 5 | Wick wanted her ruined, not dead. Eliminated. |
| 6 | Wound-down life is a currency and someone paid the Gentleman in it. |
| 7 | Small, low, four-footed, tailed, breath held. **Writes the name and strikes it out.** |
| 8 | Asks his teacher the question, gets a true answer, declines to hear it. Dreams the sewing: a grey familiar stood at her skirts. Does **not** know what that familiar paid. |
| 9 | His own ward lifted mirror-wise; a woman gone. **Writes the name and leaves it.** Then the vault docket: eight lives, *write down that he offers* — **motive, for the first time**. |
| 10 | The precedent exists and the bell is findable. Fourteen nights to the neap. |
| 11 | Names Bodkin on the record as a surviving familiar. Reads her confession. Learns the stitch cannot be taught — **he is what I am**. |
| 12 | Everything. |

**Ash tells no other character the name between chapter 9 and chapter 12.** He
carries it alone. That silence is the last third of the book.

# Chapter audit: Prologue — The Night of the Kettle

Baseline: 3015 words. Em-dashes: 15 (~5.0 per thousand — at the ceiling, do not
add).

---

## 1. Defect list

### D2 — the joke that is not a joke

> "The arrangement works, and has worked for fifteen years, which is longer
> than any dog on this street has lasted, and I have seen off three."

The owner's named example. Ninth sentence of the book and the narrator is
boasting about outliving dogs. Not funny; establishes a bully the rest of the
book never bears out; and it burns the single most valuable fact in the
paragraph — *fifteen years* — as a scoreboard instead of as an age. **Cut, and
convert the fifteen years into what they actually are: old.**

> "Nothing followed. Nothing was missing. Ash: one. The weather: nothing."

Scoreboard joke, named in the spine doc. Also a lie about the scene — he lost
that fight and fled; a scoring line lets him win it on points. **Cut.**

> "Voles say what flowers cannot."

Borderline. It is a real cat-thought and it is one line, so it survives, but it
is the only performed epigram left in the opening and it earns its place only
because the vole is a gift and the gift matters at the end.

### D3 — the gratuitous dark image

> "wound around wrists and doorposts and the necks of sleeping children"

The sentence's job is *threads are on everything*. It reaches past two mild
nouns to babies' throats for a shiver, and it makes the narrator sound like he
enjoys the frisson. **Replace "the necks of sleeping children" with
"gateposts."**

### D4 — stated game mechanics

> "I put every nature I own against it, the red and the green and the black,
> and I even spent the pale, the moonlight-colored strength that is hers"

A stat block in the middle of a fight. It enumerates a system (three natures
plus a special resource) at the exact moment the reader should be feeling a
fifteen-year-old cat losing. **Rewrite as sensation:** he fought with
everything he had including the thing of hers he carries, and felt it go.

> "I remember the pale strength going out of me in one bright useless flare"
> plus the quoted lesson "spend everything, it is not for keeping"

The pale is load-bearing and stays, but this is the second full statement of it
in three pages and it arrives pre-glossed. Keep the *going* — the sensation of
something of hers leaving him — and drop the naming and the instruction.

### D1 — unearned aphorism / image plus gloss

> "let it say I was happy, and did not know it, which is the only way anyone
> ever is."

The clause after the comma is a maxim about all persons everywhere, appended to
a true and specific thing. The specific thing is better alone. **Cut the
gloss.**

> "There is no ninth any more. Nobody remembers when that changed, and nobody
> finds that strange, and the not-finding-it-strange is itself the strange
> part."

The third clause explains the second. Image plus gloss. The plant works better
unexplained — a missing bell nobody misses. **Cut the gloss clause.**

> "Cats keep accounts. It is most of what we do, between meals."

Species aphorism, and the "between meals" tag is dryness performed. The book's
accounting theme is carried by actual accounting, not by a motto. **Cut.**

> "and the lie I told myself about not *wanting* to chase it weighed nothing at
> all. / Almost nothing."

This is good and stays — it is a fact about the speaker, and the wisp scene
cashes it two pages later. It is the one place the reach is earned.

### Future-telling

> "I did not know yet what work it was about. I have had a long time since to
> learn, and the learning is this book."

Narration announcing the book's shape. Standing prohibition. **Cut the second
sentence.**

> "The distinction matters to the things themselves, I am told, and it is about
> to matter a great deal to me."

Telegraphs the fight one sentence before it. **Cut the second clause.**

> "I am going to tell you what I chose, and then I am going to spend the rest
> of my lives wondering about it."

Half of this is throat-clearing that pre-scores the choice for the reader. The
regret lands harder if he simply reports the route and the reason. **Trim to
the report.**

### Repeated motifs

- **"The arrangement works."** Said at line 9 and again at line 27, plus
  "the arrangement of my entire life, cancelled" in ch1. Rule: a motif is said
  once. **Keep one instance in the prologue.** (The ch1 use is the payoff.)
- **"like a cat at a mousehole"** (fog, para 2) and **"a hair caught in a
  comb"** and **"like the city buttoning its coat"** — three similes in the
  first two paragraphs, one of them per paragraph is the rule. The buttoning
  coat is the best; the mousehole is a cat-simile from a cat, which is a
  tautology dressed as observation. **Cut the mousehole.**
- "Fifteen years" is stated four times. Twice is enough once it means age.

### Inconsistency with the bible

- **Ash's age is never a body.** He is fifteen and he stalks across a roofline,
  fights a wraith, sprints eleven streets and climbs a drainpipe with no cost
  anywhere. Timeline and character sheet both say he had privately retired.
  Nothing in the chapter knows this. **This is the largest defect in the
  chapter and it is an absence, not a sentence.**
- **The window is merely "Dark."** Per the spine (fix B) and the timeline
  (dusk, murder night), *she closed her shutters, which she has never done.*
  The chapter's current version cannot carry the chapter-1 candle inference —
  an hour of light in an unshuttered parlor on an inhabited lane would have
  been seen. **This is the most important structural insertion in the
  chapter.**
- "the one by the pump" / lamplighter sequence is right and stays.

### Protected — not touched

The kettle and "It took three tries. Nobody will ever know." The Clerk's entire
desk sequence. "Then that is where we are going." The red kerchief.

## 2. Purpose

**What the chapter is for:** to make the reader love a small warm arrangement
in about nine hundred words, and then take it away in front of them — and to
put the reader inside an old cat's competent, unhurried evening so that the
speed at which he loses everything is measured against how slowly he does
everything else.

**By the end the reader must FEEL:**
- fondness for a domestic routine of exactly two creatures, one of whom is
  already gone by the time we meet her;
- the specific dread of small wrongnesses accumulating in a familiar route —
  quiet pigeons, lamps in the wrong order, a shut window;
- that the last act (taking the kettle off the fire) costs more than the fight
  did.

**Must LEARN:**
- magic here is needlework; everything alive trails a thread; witches stitch,
  cats only see;
- Elspeth Vane is the Needle of Hollowmere and Ash is her cat of fifteen years;
- there are eight bells and there used to be nine;
- the Hollow Court exists, deaths are appointments, Ash has nine and has spent
  one, and no clerk has filed a nine in a very long time;
- one strand of the tether was left uncut and it runs out into the city.

**Must BELIEVE:**
- that Ash is old, and that this makes the rest of the book a cost rather than
  an adventure;
- that he is a reliable, precise reporter who does not decorate — so that when
  he *is* wrong later (the candle, the shutters, the name), the reader trusts
  the reporting and blames the world;
- that whatever did this was *working*, not hunting; the crime was
  professional, and Ash was beneath its notice.

## 3. Streamline plan

Word budget is fixed at 3015. Everything inserted is paid for by a cut.

1. **Open on the age.** Replace the dog boast with the fact it was standing in
   front of: fifteen years, an old cat, working nights behind him, a job that
   is now to be somewhere warm at the correct time. Costs nothing extra —
   the sentence is already there, it is just spending its words on dogs.
2. **Let the age colour the vole.** He does not sprint; he goes low, waits
   longer than he used to have to, and takes the vole because patience is what
   he still has. Keep "professional" — it becomes true rather than arch.
3. **Weigh one jump.** On the rooftop road home, a gap he used to take without
   thinking, taken now with a look first. One sentence. This is the character
   sheet's "looks before he leaps" made physical, and it plants her four-second
   rule that pays off at the long way home.
4. **Cut the two glosses and the two future-tellings** (bell, happiness, "the
   learning is this book", "about to matter a great deal to me"). ~70 words
   recovered.
5. **Fix the thread inventory** — gateposts, not children.
6. **Rewrite the wraith fight as a body, not a stat sheet.** He is old; the
   thing is above his weight; he spends the thing of hers and feels it go. Cut
   the scoreboard. The retreat stays and is not apologised for — it is the
   intelligent thing and it is also the thing an old cat does.
7. **The shutters.** Replace "Dark. / Her window is never dark." with the
   closed shutters and fifteen years of her coming to that window, and let him
   fail to place it and read it as *she is out*. Do not explain. This is the
   plant, and it makes the ch1 candle inference logistically sound.
8. **The eleven streets cost him.** The sprint home is the most violent thing
   his body does in the chapter; it should be reported by the body afterwards,
   at the sill, in half a line. Not feebleness — arithmetic.
9. **De-duplicate "the arrangement works."**
10. Leave the Clerk, the kettle, the kerchief and the last line untouched. They
    are the chapter's spine and they already work.

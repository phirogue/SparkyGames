# Chapter audit — 04, *The City Talks*

Baseline: 2708 words, 14 em-dashes, 3 "the way" similes.

---

## 1. Defect list

### D4 — stated game mechanics (highest priority; the owner named this one)

> "Banked shine is what the pale leaves behind it when it is spent: the bright
> grit at the bottom of the purse, no good for bread or rent, and the only coin
> the desk will take off a walk-in."

A currency, defined for the reader in a standalone expository paragraph, with a
purse and a denomination and a rule about who accepts it. This is the card
game's wallet UI in prose. It is also an "the only X that Y" construction (D1
detection pattern) stacked on top of the mechanic.

**Verdict:** cut the definition entire. The Clerk taking a fee off a walk-in
stays — a fee is a fact, a currency is a system. Ash can be poorer without the
reader being told the name of what he is poorer in.

> "my banked shine lighter by a filing fee"

Keep the fee, drop the named resource. Something of hers went and he felt it go.

> "So I lay there humiliated in a place beyond anatomy, and one entry richer:
> the desk below keeps its own calendar, and nothing I did to myself by
> carelessness would ever move an appointment up."

The second half is a stated rule of the death system — the reader is being told
the respawn policy. The first half ("humiliated in a place beyond anatomy") is
good and true. Keep the humiliation, keep the fact that the desk refused him,
drop the policy statement.

### D1 — unearned aphorism

> "...so I went and asked everyone in the city who cannot keep a secret,
> starting with the ones who keep them best."

The paradox is the whole content. It is also incoherent — the sentence says the
rats both cannot and can keep secrets, in eleven words, because the shape
demanded a turn. Nothing in the chapter pays it off.

> "A rat's silence is a price list. So is a rat's phrasing."

The first sentence is a real deduction and belongs to the case. The second is
the same figure said again for rhythm — two figures, one paragraph, and the
second does no work the first did not.

> "Nobody explained it. You might as well ask a fishwife to explain salt."

Gloss on an image that has already landed. "The water keeps everything but a
bell" is protected and needs no help; the gloss tells the reader how to take it.
Trim to the shorter form.

> "I am given to understand the minute stands to this day, and that it is the
> only thing in the archives of the Court of Whiskers with a smile in the
> pawwriting."

"The only Y that Z." Charming, and the charm is doing the work of the content.
The minute standing is the fact; the smile is decoration on decoration.

> "A season of learning what this city does with knives, and the sharpest thing
> in it turned out to be a defective hinge in a lease."

Gloss immediately preceding "Paper has teeth. I filed the lesson where I file
weapons," which is protected and is the same thought said better. Image plus
gloss, and the gloss is the weaker half.

> "He never learned any of it. That is the point."

"That is the point" is narratorial throat-clearing outside the allowed handful.
The fact lands alone.

### D2 — the joke that is not a joke

> "The court laughed the way courts laugh. He watched me not laugh."

The first clause is performed dryness that says nothing about *this* court. The
second clause is the real observation and is better without it.

> "I went down three storeys in the company of the gutter, which at least had
> the grace to go first."

Ash scoring a point during his own death. It is the exact register the bible
bans, and it costs the chapter the only moment where his age can be made real.
(See §2 and §3.)

> "From a Postmaster, before the whole Perch, that is a confession with
> plumage."

Performed wit. The confession is already strong; "with plumage" tells the reader
it was clever of the narrator to notice.

> "with the build of a mitten and the conviction of a fishwife"

Two figures in one clause. "Build of a mitten" is exact and Tansy-shaped; the
fishwife half is generic and the fishwives already had their scene twenty lines
earlier.

> "at dawn he sat down on his own threshold with it and cried like a well-fed
> child"

"Well-fed" is reaching for a specifying detail that specifies nothing. The man
crying on his own step is the whole event.

> "The city was talking again. I had never in my life been so glad to be
> insulted."

Image (pigeon insults him) plus two glosses. Warm, but the second gloss is the
narrator applauding the first.

### D3 — gratuitous dark image

> "purring faintly to itself, which is the loneliest sound I have heard in a
> city that contains the Hollow Court"

The purr alone on a shelf is already the saddest thing on the page. The
superlative reaches past it for a shiver, and drags the Hollow Court in to
borrow atmosphere it has not earned in this scene.

> "with that word sitting in my chest like swallowed lead"

Dead figure, and the paragraph already has its figure (*quartermaster*, which is
excellent and load-bearing).

### Future-telling

> "and I have been advised by the Court of Whiskers to consider what that will
> someday cost me, and I have considered it, and I find I would pay it again."

Explicit forecast of a future bill. "I would pay it again" is the good half and
survives without the advertisement.

### Inconsistency / unresolved referent

> "Find the teacher, she said."

First words of the chapter, across a chapter break. "She" is Merrow (ch. 3,
final line), but on this card, after three chapters of a dead witch, the reader
resolves "she" to Elspeth. House rule: referents resolve where they appear.

### Repeated motif (cross-chapter)

> ch4: "I do not believe in prosperous coincidences."
> ch5: "Coincidence, she used to say, is what mice call carelessness."

The same thought twice in two chapters. Ch4's is Ash's, plain, and earns its
place by immediately preceding the audit that proves it. Ch5's is an aphorism
put in Elspeth's mouth with no content (mice call nothing carelessness). Keep
ch4's; kill ch5's.

### The chapter's real hole: Ash's age

Ash is fifteen. He dies in this chapter and the manuscript treats it as an
equipment failure: "Wet iron, old fixings, a cat with his head full of seed."
Every cause is external. Nothing in the fall knows how old he is.

This is the book's best available site for the first honest acknowledgement that
he is spending a retirement — a gutter he has used four hundred times, a body
that no longer clears what it used to clear, and the four seconds of looking
that she taught him, taken and then not believed.

## 2. Purpose

**What the chapter is for:** it turns the murder from an event into an
*operation*. Ash goes out asking, and what comes back is not a suspect but a
logistics trail — seed hauled up before the night, a cart nobody booked, a
gentleman nobody books. Somebody provisioned the silence in advance. That word,
*quartermaster*, is the chapter.

**What the reader must learn:**
- The city once paid the Mere a tithe: the first of everything. It was normal.
- There was a ninth bell, and it is gone, and it was pawned, not lost.
- The murder night's quiet was bought, days early, in bulk, by something with
  hands.
- A cart went down the lane, and a gentleman walked behind it.
- Paper is a weapon in this city, and Ash can use it.

**What the reader must feel:**
- The Ratsmeet: the warm comedy of a competent bureaucracy, then the floor
  dropping out when the Grey Boss names the tithe and every old rat in the room
  touches its whiskers and Ash is the only one who needed telling.
- The fall: not shock — *recognition*. He is old. He knew he was old. He jumped
  anyway.
- Tansy: unmixed joy, and the first thing in the book that is his rather than
  hers.
- Pell: the moral baseline. A decent man saved without ever finding out, which
  is the book's argument about what warmth is for.

**What the reader must believe by the end:** that the killing was planned by
someone patient, provisioned, and local — and that Ash, who cannot fight, has
found the one weapon he can actually lift.

---

## 3. Streamline plan

1. **Resolve the opening referent.** Name Merrow.
2. **Kill the paradox** in the opening sentence; keep the plan.
3. **Ratsmeet:** cut "the way courts laugh"; cut the doubled price-list figure;
   trim the fishwife gloss off the protected bell saying. The Grey Boss's tithe
   speech and the whiskers beat are untouched.
4. **The fall — rebuild.** Replace equipment failure with misjudgement. He takes
   his four seconds, reads the jump as he has read it four hundred times, and is
   wrong about his own body rather than about the gutter. This is the chapter's
   new emotional centre and it costs no net words because the performed joke
   goes.
5. **The desk — de-mechanise.** Keep the Clerk entire, keep REFUSED, keep the
   sticking door, keep the fee. Delete the definition paragraph and the stated
   respawn rule. Replace with the sensation: something of hers, less of it.
6. **Pigeons — audit the jokes.** Keep: election by hat, feeding the electorate,
   Toby sat on by his own electors, the beaks-full confession. Cut: "confession
   with plumage," "swallowed lead," the doubled closer after the rude pigeon.
7. **Tansy — protect entire.** Trim only the second half of the mitten figure
   and the future-telling tail of the last paragraph. Her dialogue is untouched:
   "Yes. We live alone." is the model the character sheet is built from.
8. **The escrow** stays as craft — reading a contract and finding an unconveyed
   loop is lawyering, not a puzzle. Thin the aphorism density around it
   ("flawlessness is his signature", "exactness cuts both ways" — keep one).
9. **Pell** — cut "well-fed", cut the knives/hinge gloss, cut "That is the
   point." "Paper has teeth. I filed the lesson where I file weapons" is
   protected and lands harder with nothing in front of it.
10. **Budget:** at or under 2708 words, at or under 14 em-dashes. The fall
    rebuild is the only place that gains lines; everything else is subtraction.

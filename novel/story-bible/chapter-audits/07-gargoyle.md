# Chapter audit — 07, The Gargoyle and the Ghost

Baseline: 3,832 words, 20 em-dashes (5.2 per thousand — the only measurement in
these two chapters still over the book's ceiling).

**The honest diagnosis first.** This chapter is not padded and it is not
under-plotted. Its problem is that it is the most *admired* chapter in the book
and it knows it. Two large inventions — the blink reconstruction and the
Understudy — are both genuinely good, and both are surrounded by a narrator
performing his own cleverness at them: a superiority joke inside the sleeping
dog, three figures stacked where one would land, a gargoyle's address term worn
to transparency, and a closing sequence that states its own moral three times.

Every cut below is a cut to ornament. No scene, no witness and no beat is
removed.

---

## 1. Defect list

### Purpose check first — does the chapter earn its length?

It does, and the audit should say so plainly before it starts cutting.

The chapter must deliver **the parameters** (small, low, four-footed, tailed,
breath held) and **the name written and struck out**. Everything between is
tested against that:

| Element | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Sootbeard's report | **Earns.** Places the crossing on the murder night, on the clean route from Gravamen to Needle Lane, and prices the blink as a purchase — the direct receipt for the Gentleman's spool. |
| Rain barrel / dog / frost | **Earns.** These *are* the parameters. Three witnesses without eyelids is the plot, not the decoration. |
| Carried to the Tatterstage | **Earns, at half its current length.** It is the chapter's only warmth and its only view of what Ash has lost. It does not need to be the size it is. |
| The Tatterstage / the Understudy | **Earns.** She dates the technique at forty years, identifies the first victim as a stitcher (Cardew's *twice before*, now evidenced), establishes the bought fog, and supplies the only eyewitness the case will ever have. Without her the chapter has a method and no history. |
| The prompter, the chalk, the performance | **Earns.** Ash has to *do* something to get the testimony, and unpicking her rehearsed error is a rehearsal of the whole book's argument about accounts stated correctly. |

Nothing gets cut for existing. Things get cut for showing off.

### D2 — the joke that is not a joke / Ash scoring points

> "I sat with him --- **this is a thing cats can do, and I will not be
> explaining it to a species that cannot**; it is called listening, and you sit
> *downwind of the dream* ---"

The narrator stops a murder reconstruction to sneer at the reader's species.
This is the "performed dryness" the bible names, and it is the exact failure
mode of the dog boast in the owner's own D2 example. *Downwind of the dream* is
excellent and survives on its own.

> "resident in a nursing-house whose matron enforces a no-cats ordinance **with
> the zeal of a woman promoted past her judgment**."

A stranger with two clauses of existence, insulted on the way past. The book is
"funny because it is indifferent, never because it is cruel"; this is cruel
about a working woman for a beat of dryness.

### D1 — unearned aphorism and self-glossing

> "*Struck out is not unwritten. A struck entry is still an entry...*
> **That is the truest sentence in this file and I have never once got around
> it.** You cannot un-know a thing by drawing on it."

The italic entry lands. The sentence after it awards it a rosette. Then:

> "...and the reason I did not ask a clerk is **the whole of my character in
> this matter**."

Third statement of the same point in one paragraph: the entry says it, the
narrator confirms it, then the narrator diagnoses himself. Two of the three go.

> "A suspect you do not love costs you nothing to suspect, **and an entry that
> costs the filer nothing is not evidence.** It is comfort with a docket number
> on it."

Three aphorisms in a row, all making the same argument. Keep the first and the
last; the middle one is the joint between them.

### D3 — no offences

The chapter's darkness is a woman dying mid-line and a theatre going quiet.
Nothing reaches past what the story needs. This is the cleanest chapter in the
book on D3 and nothing here should be softened.

### D4 — no offences

No named strengths, no purse, no scoreboard. The "account" language belongs to
Sootbeard, whose entire character is a sentry who has never drawn on his own
watch — *"A monument's watch is its account... I am the richest unguarded purse
in this city."* That is a character's metaphor about himself, arriving at "I
wish to report a theft." It is worldbuilding and it stays.

### The repeated tic — the gargoyle's vocabulary

"Small colleague" appears **five** times: lines 17, 21, 47, 67, and — worse —
in the Understudy's mouth at 113. Alongside it, Sootbeard's diction is
interpolated into Ash's own summary sentence twice inside a single line:

> "*the counts are rising, small colleague; a sentry reports trend as well as
> incident*"
> "*which runs four minutes slow, adjust accordingly*"

Ration to one interpolation. The tower's four-minute lag is already stated twice
in chapter 6, so that is the one to go. Keep the trend note — the fog counts
rising is evidence, not flavour.

### The Understudy's rule — currently broken once

The rule is: **she speaks only in borrowed lines until her last, which is her
own.** Checked line by line:

- Line 81, *"Enter the cat, which is more than the management deserves"* —
  correctly marked as borrowed, "from some old comedy". **Kept.**
- Line 93, *"No, no, upstage, the shadow, always the shadow"* — **breaks the
  rule.** It is in quotation marks and italics, but it is not borrowed from
  anything: it is her own stage direction, in her own words, in the middle of
  the chapter. Fix by making it visibly somebody else's line, carried for the
  purpose.
- Lines 99–113, the exit speech — **correct, and this is the payoff.** It must
  stay entirely her own, which is why "small colleague of the wings" at 113 has
  to come out: it is the one borrowed thing in the only unborrowed speech she
  has, and it is borrowed from a gargoyle.

Everything else she "says" in the chapter is narrated, not quoted, which is
correct.

### One figure per paragraph — the stacked paragraphs

- **Line 73:** "a parlor the size of a guild-hall where the kettle has been
  screaming for forty years" + "a cocooned moon in its hoist-bag" + "visible the
  way the drowned are dry" — three figures in four lines. Worse, the first one
  spends **the kettle**, which is protected in its four beats and must not be
  loaned out as a simile for a building.
- **Line 41:** "pressed in its ferns like a flower in a book" + "with a jolt
  like a misjudged jump" + "out of a professionalism older than any regiment."
  Keep the jolt — it is the chapter's one moment of Ash recognising himself in
  the suspect, and it is a *body* image, which the book owes him.
- **Line 37:** "dreams keep drafts" + "downwind of the dream" + "the way sleep
  remembers a fly" + "an interruption with a silhouette."
- **Line 65:** "gilt gone the color of weak tea" + "like a healed socket." The
  socket is about the missing gargoyle and stays.

### Narratorial throat-clearing

> "(wrong, he noted, lit wrong for weeks now; he had logged each error; **I
> could have wept for the wasted vigilance of him**)"

Narrating the feeling the fact already carries — a gargoyle logging errors for
weeks with nobody to file to is the whole sentiment, delivered.

> "A theft of attention. The night of it, I confirmed against my ledger **with
> the cold going down my spine**, was the murder night."

Same defect, one line long.

### Motif wear

"Ledger" / "chest-ledger" runs five times (35, 51, 125, 127, 131) against a
book-wide budget of six. Line 127 — *"The ledger, having opened that page, would
not shut it politely"* — is pure connective tissue between "and am writing this
book" and "So I wrote the name," and those two lines are stronger butted
together.

### Age — the chapter's real omission

Ash has died once truly and been refused twice; he is fifteen. In this chapter
he sits out a whole turning of the fog on a stone ledge in the cold, is carried
across the city at two in the morning, climbs into a fly-gallery on external
ironwork, and stands a full scene under a lamp — and none of it registers
anywhere in his body. The carried-at-parade-height beat is the one place the
chapter admits he has a body at all, and it admits it as tenderness rather than
as cost.

The two places to pay it: **the fly-gallery climb** (before he sees her) and
**the empty stage afterwards** (before the sum). Plain, unpitying, two sentences
each at most.

### Length — the carrying

Not a defect, a proportion. Lines 55–63 spend about 250 words getting Sootbeard
to the theatre. The protected content inside it is small and exact: a cat
carried at parade height in a gargoyle's two fists, and *"the indignity had
somewhere in fifteen years become a thing I missed the way you miss a
language."* The setup paragraph around it (the two-sentence night-side story,
the sold-for-hardcore gargoyle, the fringe comparison, "it cost me what remained
of my composure") can do the same job in two thirds of the words.

## 2. Purpose

**What the chapter is for:** it is where the case stops being about a night and
becomes about **forty years** — and where Ash first knows the answer and refuses
it in his own handwriting.

By the last line the reader must:

- **Learn the parameters**, assembled from three witnesses that cannot lie
  because they cannot testify: *small, low, four-footed, tailed, breath held for
  the crossing.* The reader must reach the answer one beat before Ash writes it.
- **Learn the technique is forty years old**, and that the Tatterstage killing
  was a stitcher removed and a bystander given back as change. This is the first
  time Cardew's "twice before" has a face, a method and a motive-shaped hole.
- **Believe the arithmetic that follows**: too young, too new, too mortal —
  until what is left is a short list, and cats are on it, and Ash is on it, and
  the creature who gave him a name in an alley is on it.
- **Feel** the difference between Sootbeard, who will not log a conclusion his
  eyes were shut for, and Ash, who writes the conclusion and then draws a line
  through it. Both are professionals refusing to state something. Only one of
  them is being honest.
- **Feel**, in the Understudy, the book's whole thesis rehearsed in miniature: a
  scene that cannot end because the account of it is wrong, ended by a stranger
  who stands where the record says to stand rather than where grief says to
  stand. Ash fixes her account and then falsifies his own, in the same hour, on
  the same stage.

**Believe by the end:** the case is now larger than Ash's grief, and Ash is the
only obstacle left in it.

---

## 3. Streamline plan

1. **Ration the gargoyle's vocabulary.** "Small colleague" from five to two.
   Cut the four-minutes-slow interpolation (already twice in ch. 6); keep the
   rising-counts one.
2. **Cut the weeping aside and the spine-cold**, and the sneer at the reader's
   species inside the dog. Keep *downwind of the dream* standing alone.
3. **Thin the stacked figures**: kettle-simile out of the dead theatre; flower-
   in-a-book out of the frost; weak tea out of the Tatterstage front; two of the
   four out of the dog.
4. **Compress the carrying** — setup paragraph to two thirds, and cut "it cost
   me what remained of my composure," which pre-announces the joke. Keep parade
   height, keep the fists, keep *missed it the way you miss a language*, keep
   "me." on its own line.
5. **Fix the Understudy's rule** at line 93: her mid-scene direction becomes a
   borrowed line, visibly somebody else's. Remove "small colleague of the wings"
   so her own speech is entirely her own and ends on *"And so were you."*
6. **Pay the age** twice: the fly-gallery climb costs him rests he did not use
   to need; the stage afterwards costs him the time it takes to get up.
7. **Cut two of the three statements of the struck-entry moral**, and the middle
   aphorism of the three at the Court of Whiskers paragraph.
8. **Cut the connective ledger sentence** so "am writing this book" runs
   straight into "So I wrote the name."
9. **Em-dashes: 20 down to under 20**, aiming at the ceiling of 5 per thousand.
   Most of them are parentheses around asides that this pass is deleting anyway.
10. **Do not touch**: the report in sentry form and "I wish to report a theft";
    the rain-barrel, sleeping-dog and frost-pane reconstruction and the
    parameters; "I do not log conclusions my eyes were shut for"; "The house is
    not empty. It auditions."; the Understudy's exit speech including *"the
    purchase did not cover the dying"*; *"Struck out is not unwritten"*; the
    final paragraph and its lapsed forbidding.

**Budget.** The chapter can afford to lose more than chapter 6 and should. Land
under 3,832 words and under 20 em-dashes.

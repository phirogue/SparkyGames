# Chapter audit — 05, *The Ward That Failed*

Baseline: 3741 words, 9 em-dashes, 9 "the way" similes — the highest simile
density in the early book. Longest chapter of the two. It can afford to lose
the most.

---

## 1. Defect list

### The chapter's central failure: the deduction is not staged as a deduction

The book's first great elimination lives here. Wick wanted her **ruined,
dependent and owned — never dead**, because a dead seamstress mends nothing. It
is all present, but it arrives as one long speech and one summary line, and the
*shape* of it — motive established, motive inverted, suspect cleared by the very
thing that damns him — is left for the reader to assemble.

Worse, the moral squalor is underspecified. The text never says the thing that
makes Wick contemptible in one stroke: **he bought every debt she had in one
week, to convert a creditor of conscience into a tenant who could not stop.**
She could withdraw a favour any evening she liked. He wanted a woman who could
not say no. That is the sentence, and the chapter does not have it in that form.

**Verdict:** the speech stays and gets sharper; the elimination gets stated as
an accounting, in Ash's own register, in short lines.

### Chronology — must be reconciled against the timeline doc

> "Thirty-one years I have sat at this desk knowing the whole house stands on a
> favour..."

> "...I am the man who has fed the thing behind it for thirty-one years, and my
> father the nine before that."

The timeline is authoritative: the great sewing is forty years back; **Elspeth
warded the Wickhouse for two years before her death**; Lady's Day falls most of
a season out from the murder. Thirty-one years of standing on her favour
contradicts a two-year ward, and it wrecks the motive besides — a man who has
lived on a free ward for thirty-one years has no reason to move against her
*this* spring.

**Reconciliation:** the *feeding* is generational (the wax has gone to that wall
since his father's time); the *ward* is two years old. He has known for two
years that his house stands on a favour, and that is exactly why he bought her
paper this spring. The fix repairs the arithmetic and supplies the missing
urgency in the same stroke.

This also lands on the chapter's opening:

> "The wall held more evenings than that book has pages. I could name seasons in
> it the book never saw: that hard winter; that flood-spring; that autumn she
> came home smelling of pond-water..."

Written to make the ward ancient. Against the timeline it is false, and the
truer version is better: two years of nightly work produced that wall. The awe
belongs to the density, not the age.

### D4 — stated game mechanics

> "They come from sleeping in dangerous places: some economy of the pale
> strength, spending itself as memory when the body will not spend it as
> anything else."

A trigger condition and a cost model for the Remembered Days. This is a design
note. The bible allows the pale only experientially.

> "at the last crossing the pale strength again, spent freely"
> "I spent the red all at once."

Resource language twice on one page — a thing with a name, a quantity and a
spend verb. The red is instinct and the pale is hers; neither is a wallet.

> "The keeper was slow, and enormous, and lit, and that was the reading of it: a
> thing of wax serves the flame it carries, and ten thousand flames in tiers
> make a weather of rising air, and a cat who cannot out-muscle a cathedral can
> out-\emph{draft} one."

This is the boss-fight solve, stated as a rule and then executed. "That was the
reading of it" announces a puzzle; "out-draft" is a mechanic with a verb. The
image itself — a poured-tallow guardian that softens in its own heat — is fine
and should stay. It should read as **craft**: a cat who has spent a season
around a witch's work knows what heat does to wax, and walks the thing until its
own hall undoes it. Observation, not solution.

> "A stitch of hers, standing, holds what she paid into it: an evening banked in
> every hand's length."

Borderline — "banked" echoes the currency being cut from ch4. The deduction it
serves (**undone is not refunded**) is load-bearing and stays; the banking verb
goes.

### D1 — unearned aphorism

> "Coincidence, she used to say, is what mice call carelessness."

Shape of wisdom, no content — mice call nothing carelessness, and the sentence
would survive being moved to any chapter unchanged, which is the detection test.
It also duplicates ch4's plainer "I do not believe in prosperous coincidences."
Cut.

> "I know true books now; I have been schooled by rats and magpies, the finest
> forensic faculty in the city."

Superlative construction, and the joke is Ash congratulating himself mid-scene
while a man confesses.

> "It did not have habits so much as tides."

Second figure in a paragraph that has already given us "the mild interest of
deep water," which is better.

> "I have met worse in this city. I am not sure I have met colder."

A rating. It glosses a speech that has just done the work itself, and it lets
the reader off — Wick's own arithmetic is more damning than any adjective Ash
can attach to it.

> "It took me the rest of my lives to learn what the truth is for."

Forecast of a later revelation, dressed as retrospection. Standing prohibition:
narration reports what happened, never what the book will do.

### Motif twice

> "In the order I lived it, that was an old cat's sensible fear of a bad
> building..."
> "...and in the order I lived it, that was only the fog."

The construction is good exactly once. Twice in one chapter turns a confession
of hindsight into a tic, and the second is the better placed of the two.

### "The way" similes — nine, and the chapter needs three

| # | Simile | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "the way you know a rich man's back" | cut — vague |
| 2 | "the way you glance at a debt" | **keep** — Bodkin, thematic, exact |
| 3 | "the way she did when the work was long" | keep (plant; pays off at the wall) |
| 4 | "the way you feel weather" | cut |
| 5 | "the way she herself lifted stitches when a hem displeased her" | **protect** — this is the murder |
| 6 | "The way my paws had wanted to run her door-ward" | keep — not a simile, a callback |
| 7 | "warm the way his hall is warm" | keep — it is the character |
| 8 | "the way another man might cross himself" | cut |
| 9 | "the way a lane goes quiet when the lamp at the end of it dies" | cut — Merrow's page needs no help |

### D2 / D3 — smaller

> "like a comet with dignity problems"

Performed. The paragraph already has children pointing and a watchman blessing
himself, which are funnier because they are witnesses.

> "in a voice like a wick drowning"

Reaches for the dark in a book whose lake takes people. The wax keeper is gentle
and the gentleness is the unnerving part.

> "the room behind the remaining doors gets darker by exactly that much light"

Image plus its own gloss, and the arithmetic of "exactly that much" is the tell.

## 2. Purpose

**What the chapter is for:** to spend the book's most obvious suspect, and to
spend him in a way that *makes the case bigger rather than smaller*. Wick is the
man with means, motive and a wall covered in the victim's unpaid work. He is
cleared by his motive, not despite it. That is the book's first real deduction
and the reader must be able to reconstruct it afterwards from memory.

**What the reader must learn:**
- Elspeth warded the Wickhouse for two years, at night, for nothing, in secret.
- Something on the water side of that wall eats, and the guild feeds it in wax
  and calls it spoilage.
- The seam was **unpicked** — her own stitch, her own order, run backward,
  mirror-wise, faster than a needle honestly goes — by someone she never taught.
- The lifted thread comes off dead. **Undone is not refunded.**
- Wick wanted her ruined, dependent and owned, never dead. He is out.
- A cart-slot was signed out against a blind guild favour, over an inked knot,
  and the paper drains south to Gravamen.

**What the reader must feel:**
- On the sea-wall: that Bodkin is the steadiest thing in the chapter. Every word
  he says is true. That is the horror, later.
- At the wall: dread, then grief — the Remembered Day is the only warm thing in
  a cold building, and it arrives uninvited and does arithmetic on him.
- In Wick's office: cold. Not fear — *squalor*. A man explaining, in good faith,
  in true numbers, that he tried to buy a woman out of the position of being
  able to refuse him.
- On the hall steps: the blank column read aloud, twice.

**What the reader must believe by the end:** that paper did more harm here than
any knife, that Wick is contemptible and innocent at once, and that whoever did
this had her hands.

## 3. Streamline plan

Target: **under 3400 words** (≥340 off), 9 em-dashes or fewer, three or four
"the way" similes.

1. **Sea-wall:** cut simile 1. Cut "It took me the rest of my lives to learn
   what the truth is for" — keep "I put him down in the ledger as honest," which
   is the true and devastating version. Keep "In the order I lived it" here and
   cut the second instance later.
2. **Two years, everywhere.** Rewrite the shift-book paragraph so the tally and
   the wall agree: two years, and the book has all of it, and that is the
   appalling part. Delete the "seasons the book never saw" list.
3. **The Drowned:** keep "It arrived." Cut the tides clause. Convert "I spent
   the red all at once" to sensation.
4. **The Remembered Day:** delete the mechanism paragraph. Keep the trigger as
   fatigue and her scent, one sentence, no economy. The eight strays / ninth
   signature passage is the chapter's best and is untouched.
5. **The seam:** untouched except the banking verb. "Undone is not refunded" is
   the chapter's second-best line and needs nothing around it.
6. **Cut** "Coincidence, she used to say..." and the second "in the order I
   lived it."
7. **The taper walk:** cut the comet. Keep the children and the watchman and
   the week of whiskers.
8. **The wax keeper:** rewrite from rule-then-solve to craft-then-consequence.
   Same events, same courtesy, no announced reading. "She would have laughed
   until she needed the chair" is protected.
9. **Wick's office — the load-bearing rewrite.**
   - Reconcile the years (two, not thirty-one) and let the two years supply the
     motive for acting *now*.
   - Put "in one week" into the purchase, and put the conversion in his own
     mouth: creditor to tenant.
   - Keep "A favour is a thing a woman withdraws on a Tuesday... A debt is
     *held*" verbatim. It is the best speech in the chapter.
   - Cut Ash's rating of him. Let the books rate him.
10. **The elimination:** state it as an accounting, short lines, Ash's ledger
    register. Guilty of these things; innocent of the murder; and the reason is
    arithmetic, not charity. "A dead seamstress mends nothing" carries it.
11. **Merrow's reading:** cut the closing simile. The blank column read twice is
    the page; nothing else may stand on it.
12. **Close:** keep the wool run south, keep Gravamen, keep the strand at the
    window. Cut nothing there — it is the shortest passage in the chapter and
    the only one doing three jobs at once.

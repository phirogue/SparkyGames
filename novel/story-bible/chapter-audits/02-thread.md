# Chapter audit: 2, "The Thread Itself"

Baseline before rewrite: 3106 words, 14 em-dashes.
Audited against bible docs 00–04. Chapter-2 knowledge ceiling (bible 03): *the
killer reads the Weft, travels the overstreets, and cuts clean without the
right to. He notes it travels like a cat and turns the page.* Nothing beyond
that may be known, implied, or foreshadowed.

---

## 1. Defect list

### D4 — stated game mechanics (the chapter's largest defect)

> "A cat's natures are a purse, if you keep them right. Patience, the green,
> bought me the waiting at each curb while the fog breathed, and near the
> middle of the lane I felt, for the first time in my life, the bottom of that
> purse, not empty, but findable. The black bought me the stretches where being
> seen felt suddenly like a bad idea"

A resource bar with a UI. Named colours, a purse, a balance the narrator can
read off. Bible 00 lists "a cat's natures are a purse" by name as a deletion.
The deeper problem is character, not vocabulary: a cat who *spends the green*
is running a system; a cat who *waits at a curb until the wanting to move goes
quiet* is doing something, in a body, at a price. **Rewrite as things he does,
at cost, in his body.**

> "I spent a little of the pale, her Moonlight"

Same defect at one remove. Bible 00 keeps the pale — it is load-bearing to the
climax — but only *experientially*: something of hers he carries, that he feels
go, and that there is less of every time. "Spent a little of" is a withdrawal
slip. He should not be choosing it; he should feel it leave.

> "The green comes back with meat and sleep. The pale does not."

The **thought** here is protected — the one-way bequest is the best idea in the
crossing. The **nouns** are the defect. Say the ordinary strengths, not "the
green"; say what she stitched into him, not "the pale."

> "you have no pockets, or rather, you have nine, but I did not intend to spend
> the contents of my shadow on an introduction"

Inventory arithmetic. Worse, it burns a reveal: ch11 line 149 lands "Nine
pockets. Nine lives." as the moment Ash understands what he is. Counting them
in ch2 spends that for a shrug. The eerie image survives — *she sewed pockets
into his shadow* — with the number removed.

### D1 — unearned aphorism

> "That is the property of thread which makes it better company than people: it
> waits."

Sentence-shape. The comparison to people does no work, is not the chapter's
subject, and would survive being moved to any other chapter — bible 04's
detection test. Repair: "Thread waits."

> "Cutting clean is not a skill; it is a right. […] and the Weft does not
> punish that and does not forgive it. It only keeps the entry." (¶37)

Not a bad line — it is her teaching, verbatim, from ¶5 of this same chapter,
thirty paragraphs earlier. **Motif said twice.** Bible 04: a phrase is said
once. It is also said a *third* time, as the climax of the book, in ch12 ¶147
("Cutting one is a standing and not a skill, and the Weft keeps the entry").
Three statements of one law is two too many, and the ch12 use is the one that
must survive. ¶37 keeps only the new information: **this** cutter had no
standing.

> "Some accounts stay open."

Aphorism, and the account/ledger register is already carrying the whole book.

> "and I liked her enormously and permanently"

Borderline. Kept: it is short, it is a fact about the speaker, and Brindle earns
it in the next beat.

### D2 — the joke that is not a joke

> "the firm intention of never telling anyone how fast my heart had been going"

A wink from a narrator who is at that moment writing it down for whoever comes
after. Performed dryness. Replaced with the physical fact, which is better and
also does the age work.

> "it has never lost an argument in its life, which is precisely what made it
> worth arguing with"

Ash being clever about a goose before he fights it. Undercuts the beat.

> "on the fourth argument I did not lose, and left the garden unhissed-at,
> walking, which no cat in living memory has done"

**Scoreboard.** Bible 04 names this exact register ("Ash: one. The weather:
nothing"). It is also the chapter's worst structural failure — see §"Ash is
old" below.

> "once after an argument with the chimney that I maintain to this day the
> chimney started"

The warmest defect in the chapter and the hardest call. It is a performed joke
in the middle of the most serious paragraph Ash writes — the ward with a hole
in it. Cut: the warmth in that paragraph should come from four hundred patches,
not from a punchline.

> "It would learn. I was exactly an interruption."

Boast, and a promise about the future. It is also the *third* use of the
"interruption" motif in a chapter that already used it one line earlier (¶115),
after ch0 ¶127 and ch1 ¶25 — and ch11 ¶225 is the payoff. Cut; ¶115 is a better
ending for the scene.

### D3 — gratuitous dark image

Nothing rising to D3. The wisp drinking the hum off the thread ("Not trespass.
Grave-robbing") is dark and **earned**: the hum is the last of her moving in the
world, and "I am not able to write down what I did to that wisp" is Ash getting
shorter when moved, which is correct. Protect it.

### Future-telling (bible 04, standing prohibition)

> "Everything that night ran toward the Mere. I did not know that yet either."

Ash cannot know this in ch2, and the sentence exists only to nudge the reader.
Cut.

> "sewn shut below the surface by a working I would learn the price of far too
> late"

Explicit trailer for ch8/ch12. Cut and replace with a plain historical plant:
sewn shut forty years ago by somebody's needle.

### Repeated motif, internal

The dog at Number Twelve carries the same beat three times: "a silence we had
both agreed never to discuss" (¶15), "The silence between us on the murder
night went undiscussed" (¶99), "in the silence of colleagues" (¶121). Keep the
first; the third scene should *show* the watch, not name it. "Colleague" is
additionally load-bearing in ch1, ch3, ch7 and ch8 — this chapter can spare it.

"Four hundred times" appears in ch2 ¶103, ch3 ¶69, ch4 ¶47 and twice in ch11.
Ch2's is the emotional one and keeps it; ch3's is changed.

### Image plus gloss

> "Watching is not doing. I know that now better than any creature alive."

The first sentence is the image. The second tells the reader how hard to take
it, and boasts while doing it. Keep the first only. (Ch3 ¶69 restates the whole
thing again — fixed there.)

### Narratorial throat-clearing

> "Understand what it meant."

Not on bible 04's permitted list. The paragraph that follows already explains
itself.

### One figure per paragraph

The Mere-fog paragraph runs three: fog with somewhere else to be; fog declining
to leave; "the buildings on either side became opinions." The third is the
showiest and goes.

### Ash is old, and this chapter is where it must start

**This is the chapter's real defect, and it is structural rather than
line-level.** As written, the training sequence is a montage in which a cat
gets better: goose lost three times, won on the fourth, "which no cat in living
memory has done"; dog concluded in three sessions ending in a treaty. The cost
is zero, the improvement is monotonic, and nothing carries out of the sequence.

Bible 00: *the chapter 2 training stops being a montage and becomes an old
professional re-learning a trade too late, badly, and being hurt doing it.*

Four things the rewrite must supply:

1. **A split between what age took and what it did not.** Looking, patience and
   going unseen are the half of the trade that does not want a young body — he
   still has those, and they are the half he uses to win. Speed and the closing
   yard are gone, and he says so plainly.
2. **It takes longer.** Nine arguments over five nights, not four in one.
3. **It hurts, and the hurt persists.** A shoulder that does not come back,
   named once, so that it never has to be named again.
4. **A specific old-professional grief:** correctly reading a thing and being
   too slow to use what you read. That is not available to a young cat and it
   is the truest sentence the sequence can contain.

The crossing of the fog gets the same treatment: two hours of holding still,
paid for in a week of mornings.

### Continuity: the two nights of sleep do not carry

The ward mending costs him two nights on the cold hearthstone — established,
correct, protected. Then, with no acknowledgement, "That night I walked the
rounds nobody was paying for." The tiredness must be in the body that walks
those rounds.

### Cross-chapter dependency (not fixable here)

Ch1 ¶73–75 *defines* the four-strength system ("A cat runs on four strengths:
the red, the green, the black… the fourth is the pale"), ch0 ¶79 uses it, and
ch8 ¶57 has Bodkin say "no amount of the other three will cover." Removing the
system from ch2 is correct and consistent with the bible, but **the definition
in ch1 and the reference in ch8 must be dismantled by whoever owns those
chapters or the book will have a stat block with no game attached.** Flagged,
not touched.

## 2. Purpose of the chapter

**Job:** Ash stops being the survivor of chapter 1 and becomes the investigator
of the rest of the book — and the reader learns exactly what that will cost a
fifteen-year-old cat.

By the end, the reader must:

- **Feel** that the case is being carried by a body that is running out. Every
  gain in this chapter is bought with something that does not grow back: a week
  of mornings, a shoulder, two nights of sleep, and a bequest that only spends
  one way.
- **Learn** three facts about the killer and no more: it reads the Weft, it
  travels the overstreets, and it cut a thread clean without the right to. Plus
  two unremarked plants — *it travels like a cat*, noted and turned away from;
  and Ash's paws wanting to run the stitch mirror-wise.
- **Believe** that Ash can actually do this — not because he is formidable, but
  because he is patient, precise, and unwilling to have decided not to.

Structurally the chapter is: follow the thread to a dead end → buy information
with work → decide to become adequate → pay for it. The last movement is the
one the book needs and the one that was missing.

**Protected absolutely** (bible + this brief): the mirror-wise stitch beat
ending "Nothing in this book costs me more."; the cutting-is-a-right teaching
in her voice (¶5); "A familiar has one coin, and the coin is sleep."; Brindle's
"I do not fence grief."

- the purse/green/black/pale system → things done, at cost (−30)
- "better company than people" (−9)
- "buildings became opinions" (−6)
- "Understand what it meant." (−3)
- ¶37 restatement of the cutting law (−30)
- "Everything that night ran toward the Mere…" (−11)

- the chimney joke (−10)
- "I know that now better than any creature alive" (−10)
- "It would learn. I was exactly an interruption." (−8)
- "Some accounts stay open." + the treaty gloss (−15)
- "in the silence of colleagues" (−6)

**Spends** (≈150 words, all into age and cost)
- the fog crossing rewritten as two hours of holding still, priced in mornings
- the wisp night: names which half of the trade still works for him
- the training sequence rebuilt: nine arguments, five nights, a lost shoulder,
  the grief of reading correctly and arriving late
- the dog rewritten as one professional sparring with a retired one
- the two nights of lost sleep carried into the closing round

**Left alone:** ¶5 teaching, the wisp robbing the thread, the cut ending over
water, the whole Brindle scene, the ward-mending beat entire, the closing
image.

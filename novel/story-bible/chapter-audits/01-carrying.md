# Chapter audit: 1 — The Carrying and the Wake

Baseline: 2822 words. Em-dashes: 13 (~4.6 per thousand — under the ceiling, do
not add).

---

## 1. Defect list

### D1 — the unearned aphorism

> "They gave her to the tide at the turn of the evening, because the tide is
> the only thing in Hollowmere that has never been bribed."

The owner's canonical example, and it opens the funeral. It fails the test
exactly: the clause after "because" does not explain the clause before it.
Nobody bribes tides anywhere, so the boast is empty; no corruption theme in the
book pays it off; and the sentence could be moved to any chapter unchanged. It
stops the funeral dead to admire itself, in the one scene that needs the reader
looking at the bier rather than at the prose. **State the custom plainly and
let the funeral be sad.**

> "grief is nobody's business but the griever's"

A maxim tacked onto a genuinely good observation (cats sit at funerals exactly
as they sit anywhere else). The observation is the content; the maxim is the
performance. **Cut the maxim.**

> "a familiar who has outlived his witch is a piece of grief with legs and no
> one wants to catch its eye"

Two figures in one clause and the second is a wince. The behaviour — everyone
studying the floor — already says it. **Reduce to the behaviour.**

> "quiet has a price either way, and prices leave receipts"

Aphorism in the middle of the Item list, which is the one passage in the book
that should be flat. The Items are evidence; evidence does not editorialise.
**Cut the tag.**

> "a knock asks a question, and the house has already answered it"

This one is earned and stays. It explains an actual custom, in one figure, and
it is about the world rather than about all persons everywhere.

### D4 — stated game mechanics

> "The pale wants explaining, once, so the rest of this account can spend it
> without stopping. A cat runs on four strengths: the red, which is the hunt;
> the green, which is patience; the black, which is the long watch and the soft
> foot. Those three are mine: spend them and they grow back with meat and
> sleep. The fourth is the pale, moonlight-colored, and the witches call it
> plainly Moonlight: it is memory made spendable, it pays the prices the other
> three cannot, and no cat makes his own."

The clearest stat block in the manuscript: an enumerated four-resource system
with regeneration rules, delivered by the narrator as a rules explanation
("wants explaining, once, so the rest of this account can spend it"). It also
future-tells — it announces that later chapters will spend it. Worse, it sits
between the candle stub and the cork board, stopping the investigation to open
the manual. **Delete as a system.** Keep only the last, true, load-bearing
thing: it is hers, she stitched it into him, he can feel it go, and there is
less of it every time. Experiential, unnumbered, unnamed as a category.

### The plot hole — how did the witches know?

> "Three witches came at first light."

Nobody sent for them. Ash was alone in the house all night; no messenger, no
bird, no bell. The reader who asks the question gets no answer, and it
undermines Cardew, who currently appears by authorial convenience.

**Fix, per the spine:** the Circuit is nine seats holding one binding between
them; when one goes out the other eight feel the load come onto their own
hands. Cardew felt the seat go out at dusk. She came at first light rather than
at once because you do not walk Needle Lane in the dark while something is
unpicking it. Two or three sentences, in her register — unfinished, worn down —
and it does triple duty: closes the hole, explains her "Again," and explains
why the Circuit will not be investigating (they are holding what she held).

### The missing scene — why nobody else looks

Per bible 05, ch1 owes the reader two things it does not currently contain:

1. **The day-side watch comes and finds no case.** Two constables, honest,
   mildly bored, looking at an old woman who died in her chair — unmarked, no
   forced entry, nothing taken. They cannot see forty years of cut workings
   hanging at one tidy height all around them. They step over the candle stub.
   Ash watches them miss everything and understands that if he does not do
   this, nobody will. The premise stops being convenient and becomes
   inevitable.
2. **Cardew states plainly what will and will not happen** — the Circuit must
   hold the seams because there is nobody else, no institution on the
   night-side owns a killing, and she has signed closings before. Her existing
   quay speech does two-thirds of this already; it needs the seams and the
   "nobody will look" said once, in her voice.

The chapter is at its word ceiling, so these are paid for out of the four-
strengths block (~130 words), the tide aphorism, the maxims listed above, and
the trimmed condolence figures.

### Logistics — the candle

> "Somebody stood at this window, in the dark of my witch's parlor, and burned
> a fine wax candle down one inch, which is about an hour"

The reasoning is good and the reader must be able to re-read it later and see
Ash being wrong. Two problems. First, it currently reads at the seam between
inference and fact — "And out of that inch of wax I built the man who left it"
is the right move, but the paragraphs after it slide into narrated certainty
("Somebody *waited* here"). Keep the whole thing explicitly his construction.
Second, an hour of candlelight in an unshuttered ground-floor parlor on an
inhabited lane would have been seen by somebody. **The closed shutters planted
in the prologue are the answer, and Ash should reach for them here** — as
corroboration of his intruder, not as the anomaly they actually are. He uses
the true clue to build the false man. That is the whole design.

### Repeated motifs and small things

- **"thin as spite"** — used for the uncut strand in the prologue *and* again
  in the Item list here. A motif is said once. **Cut the second.**
- **"like the kettle"** in the shroud-seam ("the first stitch took me three
  tries, like the kettle") — this is a deliberate echo of a protected line and
  it is the best sentence in the chapter. It stays.
- "and nobody will ever know that either, except that I am writing it here, so
  now everyone will, and I find I do not mind" — three beats where one would
  do, and it slightly cheapens the protected original by explaining the joke.
  Trim to two.
- "I would understand later" (the Lamplighters sent four) — mild future-telling,
  but it is a factual flag on a fact, not a promise about the book. Acceptable;
  keep.
- "in a voice that ended the subject for a generation" — good, keep. It is
  about Cardew, not about the world.

### Protected — not touched

Cardew's "Who did this to the room." and "Again." and "Let him,". The whole
shroud-seam sequence on the quay. The favour-knot and "A debt. Mine. You'll
know." The kettle on the cold hearthstone. The final dream and the last line.

## 2. Purpose

**What the chapter is for:** to bury her properly, to make Ash the only
investigator in the city by demonstration rather than assertion, and to hand
him the first two facts of the case (*again*; *ninth*) from a woman who is
ashamed to be handing them over.

**By the end the reader must FEEL:**
- the funeral as a funeral — not as an occasion for the narrator's best
  sentences;
- that Ash's one competent act at the bier is the most he has ever been asked
  for and very nearly more than he can do;
- the flat, unangry frustration of watching honest people look straight at a
  murder and see an old woman who died in her chair.

**Must LEARN:**
- the Circuit is nine seats, now eight, and the survivors felt her go;
- the day-side has no case and the night-side has no constabulary;
- it has happened twice before, both stitchers, both files closed by Cardew;
- Ash was "her ninth" and she would never say what she meant by the count;
- somebody burned an hour of guild beeswax in a parlor that never held any;
- the Mere has a smell and it was in that room.

**Must BELIEVE:**
- that Ash's intruder is a reasonable construction from real evidence — so
  that the re-reader, who knows she lit the candle herself, feels the floor
  move rather than feels cheated;
- that Cardew is not obstructive but complicit and overworked, which is worse
  and better;
- that the pale is hers and finite, without ever having been told what it costs
  or how much is left.

## 3. Streamline plan

Net word budget must not exceed 2822.

1. **Open with the seat going out.** Three sentences in Cardew's register at
   the top of the chapter, so the witches arrive answered rather than
   convenient. Pays for itself against the hole.
2. **Trim the two condolence figures** in the arrival and the roofline
   paragraphs to their behaviours.
3. **Insert the day-side watch** (~200 words) between the witches leaving and
   Ash minding the room — so that he searches the parlor *after* watching two
   honest men fail to see it. His "New job" then lands as the only available
   conclusion rather than as a resolution he chose for flavour.
4. **Delete the four-strengths block entirely** (~130 words) and replace with
   two sentences of the pale as sensation, placed where it belongs: at the
   Item list, as the last thing he inventories, because it is the only asset he
   has and it is not his.
5. **Make the candle inference audibly his.** Keep "I built the man who left
   it"; carry the conditional through the paragraphs that follow; and let him
   cite the closed shutters as the reason no one on the lane saw a light. He is
   right about the shutters and wrong about the man.
6. **Replace the tide aphorism** with the custom stated plainly.
7. **Give Cardew the "nobody will look" statement** on the sea-wall, folded
   into her existing speech — seven pairs of hands holding what one woman held,
   no desk in the city that owns a killing, and she signed the closings. Her
   sentences stay unfinished.
8. **De-duplicate "thin as spite."**
9. Leave the shroud-seam, the favour-knot, the board, the dream and the last
   line untouched.

# Story bible 2: character sheets

Every character gets: who they are, what they **want** (stated, pursued), what
they **need** (unstated, often the opposite), how they **talk**, and what they
**do with their hands** when nobody is looking. If a line of dialogue could be
moved to another character's mouth without loss, it is wrong.

---

## ASH (Ashcat, of Needle Lane) — narrator

**Age.** Fifteen. That is old. He is stiff on cold mornings, he thinks before
he jumps where he used to simply jump, and he had privately retired before any
of this started. His working nights were behind him; his job was to be
somewhere warm at the correct time, and he was good at it.

**Backstory.** A stray. She took him in fifteen years ago and named him, which
is why he says he knows her hand better than his own name — she gave him both.
He has never worked a case, never held an office, never been asked for
anything. He has been *kept*, comfortably, for fifteen years, and he assumed
that was because he was loved. It was. It was also restitution.

**Wants:** to find who killed her, and to be, briefly, adequate to the moment
he was not adequate to in the parlor.

**Needs:** to learn that the warmth he lived inside was the amends for a crime
committed before he was born — and to decide whether that makes it worth less.
It does not. Getting there is his arc. Then he must do the one thing she could
not: carry the account to the creditor and say it to his face.

**The flaw that drives the plot.** He mistakes having been loved for having
been the only one loved. When evidence arrives that there was somebody before
him, he does not follow it. He is brave about hounds and cowardly about one
name, and he knows it, and he writes it down anyway.

**Mannerisms.** Names the parts before he begins, the way she began a working.
Keeps his ledger in his chest and its worst entries in a smaller hand. Looks
before he leaps and times it — her rule, four seconds. Grooms with terrible
thoroughness when frightened. Cannot pass a warm rectangle. Takes the long way
when he is afraid and calls it professionalism. Says "Item:" when he is trying
not to feel something.

**Voice.** Precise, dry, formal when hurt. He is writing a report because a
report is the only dignity available to him. He is funny only when accuracy is
funny. **He never reaches for effect.** When he is moved he gets *shorter*, not
more ornate: "It took three tries. Nobody will ever know."

## ELSPETH VANE — the Needle of Hollowmere (dead before page one)

**Backstory.** Forty years ago she was the only needle in the city strong
enough to close the Mere. She walked out onto the black water alone because
the working wanted the Needle and no other weight. The anchor required one
finished life. Hers was already mortgaged to the seam. Her familiar offered
his. She entered his offer in the record so that neither of them could take it
back, drew eight lives down her own needle, and came back off the water alone.

For forty years afterward she wrote *he is owed* at the head of every page and
never once walked to the sea-wall to say it out loud.

**Wants:** to re-found the seam permanently before she dies — a working that
outlasts her hands.

**Needs:** to have gone and said it. She never does. Her tragedy is moral
cowardice wearing the costume of diligence: she paid the debt forward, to
strays, in warmth and kettles, because paying it forward did not require
standing in front of the person she had hurt.

**Mannerisms.** Prices a churn-blessing to the ha'penny and chases the
ha'penny. Says "not on the table" and means thank you. Paces a hem when
thinking. Keeps her real accounts in the margins of other books. Never blots —
except in the confession, where the hand shakes.

**Voice.** Impatient, practical, dry. Tender only sideways and never on
purpose. "Mind the house, Ash. I am going to see about the lamps."

## BODKIN — the grey tom; her first familiar; the murderer

**Backstory.** Hers before Ash was born, nine years at her skirts. He stood to
her on the black water while she drew eight of his nine lives down her needle
into the bottom of the lake, and he did not cry out, and he did not run. Then
he walked into the fog on the one life she left him. Forty years on the
sea-wall, watching her window, mending what frayed, waiting for a court that
did not exist.

**Wants:** an accounting. Not revenge — he says so and he means it. He wants
the account *stated*, entire, aloud, by a party with standing, which is the one
thing forty years never produced.

**Needs:** to be told, by someone who has the right to say it, that he was
owed. Ash is the only creature alive who can.

**Why now.** She was about to make the seam permanent. While the seam could be
opened, his debt stayed collectible. Her renewal would have priced his waiting
at nothing, forever — and she never once thought of it that way, because she
was minding a city.

**Why he armed Ash.** A collection without a stated account is theft, and he has
never been a thief. A settlement wants a party with standing. So he built one:
found good stock, kept it alive, let it learn. He paid for his own hearing.

**Mannerisms.** Gives things exactly once — his name, a rule, a lesson — take
it or leave it. **Never lies. Not once in the book.** Half his sentences are
worn smooth and he leaves the listener to fetch the rest. Mends what he finds
frayed and never mentions it. Sits where he can see the water.

**Voice.** Flat, economical, unhurried. Not menacing — *available*. The horror
of him is that everything he says is true and he will answer anything he is
asked.

**He is not a monster.** He is an old professional carrying an unpayable
invoice who finally found a desk that would take it.

## THE CLERK — the Hollow Court

**Wants:** correct filings and a quiet desk.

**Needs:** something irregular to care about. He would never say so. Ash's file
becomes the most interesting thing that has happened to him in centuries, and
the most he will ever admit is that he no longer altogether minds.

**Mannerisms.** Says things to the paper rather than to the person. Turns the
lamp up one degree, which is the outer limit of his tenderness. Two inkwells on
the bench when he is enjoying himself. A small dry ceremonial pause where other
people would smile. Stamps with a paw print and never explains it.

**Voice.** Bureaucratic deadpan that turns out to have been kindness all along.
Never sentimental, never cruel. **He is the funniest thing in the book and he
is never trying to be.**

## MOTHER CARDEW — eldest of the Witches' Circuit

**Backstory.** Sixty years in the trade. Knows every stitching hand in the
city. Forty years ago she signed the closing on the Tatterstage murder, and
three winters later on Widow Sarn's, and both times nobody looked. She has
carried that since.

**Wants:** the Circuit to survive an eighth seat going empty; not to have to
open the old files.

**Needs:** to say out loud that she closed them, and to open them anyway.

**Mannerisms.** Leaves sentences unfinished — she wore the ends off them years
ago and stopped missing them. Speaks to a cat colleague-to-colleague while
everyone else studies the floor. Her knees cost her when she kneels and she
pays without comment.

**Voice.** Age carried through *incompleteness*, never through aphorism. She
does not make maxims. She stops.

## MERROW — shift-boss, the Lamplighters

**Wants:** her missing back. Lights lamps until they walk in.

**Needs:** to accept that one of them will not, and to keep lighting his lamp
anyway.

**Mannerisms.** Chalks a tally on the hall door. Stands facing the door where
bad news must come through her first. Chalk on her hands always.

**Voice.** Procedural terseness. "I answer what is asked. No more." **Strip
every epigram from her.** She is strongest when least witty; her finest moment
in the book is a flat two-line bill about Fenn.

---

## BRINDLE — the Magpie Exchange

**Wants:** a fair trade and a full ledger.

**Needs:** nothing. She is the one adult in the book who is fine.

**Mannerisms.** Head cocked, one eye at a time. Hops the rail. Watches upside
down. Holds a thing a moment longer than trade requires, then turns away —
that is the whole of her condolence.

**Voice.** Broker's patter with actual numbers in it. "Payment comes in kinds."
Will not fence grief.

---

## TANSY — the kitten

**Backstory.** Sold her purr to the Quiet Gentleman to buy her brothers out of
a flooded canal. Ash unpicked the escrow and gave it back. She moved in without
being invited and has never left.

**Wants:** to help. To be fierce. To come along.

**Needs:** nothing yet. She is the book's only unmixed joy and must stay that
way.

**Mannerisms.** Sleeps in the exact centre of things. One chewed bootlace as
her entire estate. Washes her ears through important meetings.

**Voice.** **Concrete and literal — never aphoristic.** She states the law
better than the lawyers because she says the plain version: "So you weren't
stealing time. The city owed you a sunrise, and you have the receipt." That
works precisely because it is not clever.

---

## THE QUIET GENTLEMAN — broker of intangibles

**Wants:** open accounts. He feeds, structurally, on things left unsettled.

**Needs:** he keeps one account for no reason money knows — a vial of her tears
he has never sold.

**Mannerisms.** Fog at the ankles. Gloved. Nobody remembers meeting him the
first time. His paper guards itself.

**Voice.** Contractual, precise, oddly tenseless — a document read aloud, not a
wit trading epigrams with the narrator. He does not banter.

---

## ELLERY WICK — Chandlers' Guild

**Wants:** Elspeth ruined, dependent, and owned — never dead. He bought every
debt she had in one week to convert a creditor of conscience into a tenant who
could not stop sewing his warehouse shut.

**Needs:** to be seen as respectable, which is why he stands where he can be
seen being sorry.

**Mannerisms.** Very good black. Arrives early to be observed. Keeps true
books and cannot lie in arithmetic.

**Voice.** A coward about everything except numbers.

---

## MISTRESS SHEAR — rival witch

**Wants:** the ninth seat, and the hand that would deserve it.

**Needs:** to grow up, which she does, in public, in one silent moment when she
fails the stitch in open court and lays the needle down square.

**Mannerisms.** Dressed a half-degree finer than the room. Correctness worn
like frost. Hands that hover a half-inch off the glass, wanting.

## SUPPORTING

**Sootbeard** — gargoyle, three hundred years on one street, decommissioned in
1731 and never stood down. Wants his report *received*. Speaks in sentry form:
item, item, item. Never speculates beyond his own eyes.

**The Understudy** — ghost of the Tatterstage. Wants the scene to end
correctly. Speaks only in borrowed lines until her last, which is her own.

**Pell, the Fish-Giver** — feeds every stray in Thimblefield at dawn, eleven
years, and calls them all "puss." Wants nothing. He is the book's moral
baseline and must never be made quaint.

**Fenn** — lamplighter, steadiest round of the four left. Walks into the fog
with his lamp still burning. He is the price the book actually pays.

**The Grey Boss** — rat, remembers the ninth bell and what the city used to pay
to keep it ringing. Dry, transactional, unafraid.

**The Mere** — not a monster. **A creditor.** The city ran up a bill over
generations and stopped paying it. It keeps accounts the way the sea keeps
tides. It does not hate; hate would be safer. It hopes.

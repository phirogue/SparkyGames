## 1. Cross-chapter repetition

### 1.1 Her four-seconds rule, stated three times — **FIXED**
- Prologue: *"look before you leap, Ash, it is the entire difference between
  brave and stupid"* (plant)
- Ch2: *"Her rule, from the windowsill years: the difference between brave and
  stupid is about four seconds of looking."*
- Ch12: *"the difference between brave and stupid is four seconds of looking,
  Ash. The difference between justice and spite is the same four seconds."*
  (payoff)

Ch2's is the redundant middle: it restates the aphorism with nothing added, in
a chapter that then goes on to use the practice anyway. **Fix:** ch2 keeps the
practice, drops the quotation.

### 1.2 "I looked. Four seconds. Longer." — verbatim in two chapters — **FIXED**
- Ch9 (drowning on the flats): *"the thing my whole training had been for: I
  looked. Four seconds. Longer."*
- Ch12 (standing over the sleeping Bodkin): *"I looked. Four seconds. Longer."*

Ch12's is the one the book is built toward — it is the beat Bodkin then answers
with *"I did not spend four seconds looking."* **Fix:** ch9 drops the count and
keeps *"I looked."*

### 1.3 "four seconds" as a tic — **PARTLY FIXED**
Nine occurrences across the book before this pass (prologue, ch2, ch3, ch4, ch9
×2, ch12 ×3). Cuts at 1.1, 1.2 and one decorative use in ch9 ("four seconds
spent on every timber of the frame") bring it to six, with the aphorism itself
said exactly twice. Remaining uses each do work: prologue (the Cooper's gap),
ch3 (*"I spent six"* — an age beat), ch4 (the four seconds were right, and taken
about a younger cat), ch12 ×3 (the payoff and Bodkin's confession).

**AUTHOR:** prologue *"counted the four seconds she taught me"* and ch4 *"I gave
it the four seconds she taught me"* are near-verbatim. Ch4's is load-bearing
(it kills him). If one goes, it is the prologue's — but the prologue's is also
the plant, so I left both.

### 1.4 "decided I was not an interruption" — four times — **FIXED**

## 2. The shutters chain

All three links exist and hold.

- **Prologue** plants and does not explain: *"The shutters are closed… I stood in
  the road and could not make that mean anything… So I did what anybody does with
  a fact that will not sit down. I told myself she was out."* Clean — the
  anomaly is filed as unfileable.
- **Ch1** builds the intruder out of it: *"Then I remembered the shutters, closed
  for the first time in fifteen years, and thought: of course. He closed them. He
  shut the lane out, lit his own candle, and settled down to wait for her."*
  Flagged in-text as construction, not finding (*"What follows is a thing I
  built, not a thing I found"*). Correct.
- **Ch12** reverses it: *"She went round the parlor and closed the shutters…
  Then she took the good beeswax candle out of its drawer… and stood it unlit
  where a guest coming in would have it in the eye."* Correct.

### 2.1 Ch12 contradicts the prologue's own physical fact — **FIXED**
> ch12: *"And when I came to that window at the end of it I read the black glass
> the way anybody would have read it. **Out**, I thought."*

There was no black glass to read. The shutters were shut — that is the whole
plant. **Fix:** he reads the shut leaves.

### 2.2 **AUTHOR** — bar vs. brick
Prologue: *"They are old green shutters and they fold back against the brick"*
(exterior). Ch12: *"Both leaves, and the bar across"* (fastened from inside).
Not impossible — you reach out, pull both leaves to, and drop a bar behind the
casement — but a reader who has pictured exterior shutters folding flat against
the wall may stumble. If you want it airtight, either ch12 becomes *"Both leaves,
and the latch across"* or the prologue drops *"against the brick."* I did not
guess.

Everything else in the chain agrees: fifteen years of never being closed
(prologue, ch1, ch12); her window is the parlor window; the candle stub is found
inside, under the same window, where the curtain hem brushes the boards (ch1);
the vole in ch10 is wedged *"at the corner where the shutter meets the frame."*
Ash entering by the **workroom** window on the murder night (prologue, ch1) is
consistent — only the parlor was shuttered.

### 3.1 **AUTHOR** — the one athletic beat

### 5.1 Ch10 credits Ash with having *seen* the eight — **FIXED**
> ch10: *"Her own account of the water, the working, and **the eight lights I
> watched go out at the corner of a dream**."*

He did not watch them. Ch8 explicitly withholds it, twice, and the withholding
is the chapter's engine. By ch10 he knows the number — from the docket — so the
fact is fine and only the sourcing is wrong. **Fix:** the eight the docket
counted.

### 5.2 Ch12 repeats the same error — **FIXED**
> ch12: *"It was the reverse of everything I had watched at the corner of the

Same problem, and it matters more here because the sentence is the book's last
statement about the working. **Fix:** the reversal is of what the dream withheld

## 7. Surviving defects

### 7.1 Future-telling: ch2 — **FIXED**
> *"…and I remember being irritated at my own hands and thinking nothing of it.
> **Nothing in this book costs me more.**"*

Exactly the prohibited shape — narration reporting what the book will do. The
plant is already perfect without it (*thinking nothing of it* is the whole
device). **Fix:** cut the sentence.

### 7.2 Future-telling: ch7 — **FIXED**
> *"I want that on the record exactly as it happened, **because everything left
> in this book is built on the minute that followed.**"*

Same shape, and the paragraph is stronger with the plain statement of intent
standing alone. **Fix:** cut the because-clause.

### 7.3 **AUTHOR** — performed joke, ch13
> *"She is right about everything; it is her one flaw."*

Affectionate, and it is Brindle, who has earned it. But it is Ash scoring a
point in the epilogue's summing-up, and it is the only line in ch13 that would
survive being moved to another chapter unchanged. Your call.

### 7.4 Checked and clean
- No unearned aphorisms survive. Every *X is the only Y* construction in the
  book cashes out on the page (ch11 the Gentleman's oath; ch12 the higher bench's
  *"the only honest way to lose"*; ch12 *"Take is the only verb it owns"*; ch13
  Merrow). Every load-bearing *because* explains.
- No gratuitous dark images. The two hardest — Fenn folded away standing, and
  Ash going out to open his teacher's throat — are both immediately priced by the
  narrator, which is what makes them evidence rather than shiver.
- No stated game mechanics. The pale is only ever experiential (*"I felt it go…
  there was less of her in me afterward"*); the shadow-pockets are never counted;
  the red feeling is never one of a set. The Clerk states rules **as a clerk**,
  which is the permitted form.
- **AUTHOR, low priority:** the epilogue's signature block — *"nine lives
  allotted, two spent, seven held / in trust"* — is the closest thing left to a
  stat line. It reads as a ledger sign-off, which is the book's own idiom, so I
  left it; but it is the one place a reader could see the machine.

## 8. Factual contradictions

### 8.1 Ch5 misquotes Cardew — **FIXED**
- Ch1 (spoken): *"You were her ninth, you know. She'd never say what she meant
  by the count."*
- Ch5 (Ash recalling it): *"you were her ninth, you know. She would never say
  what she meant by **keeping count**."*

A remembered quotation that does not match what was said, in a book whose
narrator's whole authority is that he transcribes exactly. **Fix:** ch5 matches
ch1 word for word.

### 8.2 Ch11 puts Nan Sarn's death in the wrong year — **FIXED**
Ch11, Cardew's testimony:
> *"Adela Marchmain… Found in her dressing room, the fourth of Hollowmas,
> **forty-one** years ago."*
> *"Nan Sarn… Found in her own kitchen **the following spring**…"*
> *"There were four hands in this city that could hold a seam. **Inside one
> year** there were two."*

Against:
- **Bible:** Marchmain in Year 0, Sarn in **Year 3**.
- **Ch1** (Cardew, same speaker): *"And Widow Sarn of Hemlock Row, **three
  winters after**."*
- **Ch13** (Cardew, same files, one season later): *"Died the fourth of
  Hollowmas, **forty** years ago"* / *"Died **three springs after**."*

Three errors in one speech: the year count, the interval, and the arithmetic
that depends on the interval. Cardew is the one character who must not get these
wrong — she signed both closings and has carried them for forty years. **Fix:**
forty years; three springs after; *inside three years there were two.*

### 8.3 Bodkin's statements — audited, all true
Every factual claim he makes in ch12 checks against the timeline:
- *"I posted it myself, kit, the gentlest slot on the whole ledger"* — matches
  the blank posting line the Clerk finds in ch9. ✓
- *"The work was done between the boiling and the scream"* — matches the
  prologue and the timeline. ✓
- *"Fifteen years I watched her wind you. Nine lives, kit."* ✓
- *"Every soul in that hall walked out of it on his own feet. The word I gave
  them was true. There was work at the Gravamen yards."* ✓ — and it does **not**
  contradict the eleven who never arrived: the hall emptied on a true word, the
  road ran past the fog, and Ash bills him precisely for the fact he kept back,
  not for a lie. The amendment *"Not emptied. Sent."* is the correct repair and
  Ash makes it on the record.
- *"It cannot lift a stitch"* — consistent with everything the Mere is shown
  doing (taking, never lifting) from ch5 onward. ✓
- The one untrue sentence he speaks (*"I have never once in forty years been a
  thief"*) is explicitly identified as untrue by the narrator, and the candle is
  the exhibit that disproves it. That is the design, not a defect. ✓

### 8.4 The murder night — who was where, cross-checked
Prologue / ch2 (Brindle's cart) / ch4 (the Grey Boss's gentleman) / ch5 (Wick's
counterfoil) / ch6 (the Gentleman's three blind favours) / ch7 (Sootbeard's
blink at four minutes past seven, by a tower that runs four minutes slow) / ch11
(the carrier's counterfoil, *street-thread, harvested, spooled*) / ch12
(Bodkin's own account) all agree with each other and with the timeline. The
seed is bought days in advance; the cart runs light down and heavy back toward
the Mere; the Gentleman walks behind it at strolling pace; the lamps are walked
in reverse; the hour is spent at tea; the candle is pinched out. **No further
contradictions of the Bodkin/Mere class found.**

### 8.5 Checked, not a contradiction
- Ash entering by the workroom window on the murder night while the parlor
  shutters are barred — consistent, and quietly explains itself.
- The strand running out from under the window past closed shutters — Weft
  threads pass through windows and gutters throughout (ch2); not a physical
  object.
- *"eight strays"* (failed bonds) vs *"eight lives"* (the anchor) — two
  different eights, never conflated in the text, and the book never lets Ash
  draw the line between the two nines until ch9. Correct.
- Ash's Court visits: prologue (kept, on the docket) → ch4 (refused) → ch6
  (refused) → ch7's tally *"three times this season, once on the docket, twice
  REFUSED"* ✓ → ch8 (refused, the fire) → ch9 (kept; the Toll). The epilogue's
  *"two spent"* is correct.

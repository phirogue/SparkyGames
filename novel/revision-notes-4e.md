# Fourth-Edition Revision — adjudication and design

Four fresh blind critics read the third edition cold and were then asked to
harshen their reviews to a 6/10 calibration (`novel/reviews-4e/`). This
edition takes the harsh round seriously: it is not polish, it is structural
work on the middle third, the ending's cost, the suspect architecture, and
the voice monoculture.

## The five structural commitments

These are the changes everything else hangs from.

**1. The book commits to dramatic irony.** All four critics: the reader has
the killer by ch. 3–7 while the text performs astonishment until ch. 11.
The fix is not better hiding — the mirror-stitch plant makes a familiar
guessable by design — it is honesty. The bodkin-name gloss moves from ch. 3
to the ch. 11 reveal; the middle is retuned as the reader's certainty
grinding against Ash's refusal to see; and in ch. 11 the narrator concedes
the reader's position outright, converting the gap into complicity
(Mystery #1, #14; Pro-Ugly 2).

**2. The middle gets a live clock.** The Lady's Day deadline dies with Wick
in ch. 5 and nothing replaces it. Fix: the seam's decay becomes a visible,
escalating countdown — one concrete symptom per chapter from ch. 4 on,
logged in Ash's ledger — and Lady's Day is *cashed*: the Hollow Court
dockets the hearing for Lady's Day, so the date set with menace in ch. 3 is
the date the case turns on (Pro #1).

**3. The ending keeps a named loss: Fenn does not come home.** The ruling
disgorges what the Mere *held*; but what the Mere *spent* is spent — and in
the last weeks, pressing against the failing seam, it drew on the freshest
takings as fuel. Fenn was spent. His lamp keeps burning in the epilogue;
the Lamplighters say it to Ash's face; Ash puts it on his own bill because
Fenn was taken during the weeks Ash contested. "Held is not spent" stays
true — that is exactly why this loss is permanent (Mystery #5, Fantasy #3,
Pro-Ugly 1's cost logic).

**4. Ash gets his anger before his mercy.** A new beat between the ch. 11
revelation and the ch. 12 settlement: Ash at Number Twelve's fence at
night, wanting to open Bodkin's throat, choosing the account-stating
instead — on camera, at real risk of doing otherwise (Pro #4).

**5. The detective extracts; the world stops donating.** The Gentleman's
ch. 11 exhibit trail is priced (traded against the knot he is owed), not
"tendered unasked." The Horologists do not summon Ash; he catches the
golden hour himself and comes knocking. Box 9-Crown's confession is shown
— half a page of Elspeth's own words — not summarized into a redaction
(Mystery #4; Young #5; Pro #6).

## Adjudication by critic

### Mystery fan (14 recommendations)
1. Cut ch. 3 bodkin gloss, move to ch. 11 — **accepted**.
2. Move motive load off the ch. 8 dreams onto findable evidence —
   **modified**: dreams stay (they are Remembered Days, paid for, in-world
   documents), but ch. 9's vault visit adds a findable corroborating record
   (the Clerk's registry of the forty-year working) so the reader competes
   on evidence before the dream is confirmed.
3. Manufacture a genuine suspect interval — **modified**: with commitment
   1, fake doubt is abandoned rather than manufactured; the senior grey
   becomes a scene about Ash *wanting* a stranger to be guilty (see 7).
4. Ash extracts what is donated — **accepted** (commitment 5).
5. Named loss stands in epilogue — **accepted**: Fenn (commitment 3).
6. Bodkin owns the sabotage in ch. 12 — **accepted**: "why did you arm me?"
   is asked and answered on the page; plus the why-now mechanism (see
   Fantasy 5).
7. Rebuild or cut the senior grey — **accepted (rebuild)**: one real scene
   (ch. 9, ropewalk roof): Ash reads him, gets him wrong on purpose,
   and knows he is doing it — the herring becomes the denial theme.
8. Give the Shear frame teeth — **accepted (light)**: the Circuit suspends
   her warrant on the planted evidence in ch. 8/9; the hearing has real
   pressure behind it.
9. Close the strand's metaphysics — **accepted**: one clause in ch. 1/2 —
   the strand ran to the stolen wards the killer walked out wearing.
10. Reconcile cart driver / scratched consignee / seed-hauler in ch. 12 —
    **accepted**, one sentence each inside the stated account.
11. Fish-Giver episode gets case-work — **accepted**: the defective-lease
    maneuver is explicitly Ash's first "paperwork is a weapon" lesson,
    recalled at ch. 9; the bank-of-the-Chandlers thread feeds ch. 5.
12. Compress ch. 10 by a third, delete the lien-law lecture — **accepted**.
13. De-dup Cardew's speech; name the second stitcher — **accepted**
    (she is named Hemlock Row's Widow Sarn, one trade, one mourner).
14. Acknowledge the reader's solve — **accepted** (commitment 1).

### Fantasy fan (12 recommendations)
1. Build the Mere — **accepted**: one page in the ch. 8 dream shows the
   pre-seam city (what the water did when it was loose; why the city is
   there — the Mere was the harbor, the wealth, the reason); one concrete
   image of its nature at ch. 9; the ch. 12 "contempt in absentia" joke is
   cut and replaced with something colder.
2. Plant the finished-life anchor rule early — **accepted**: the Clerk, in
   the ch. 9 audit: an anchor wants a life that is finished paying.
3. Ending costs what ch. 9 promised — **accepted**: Fenn (commitment 3).
4. Break the voice monoculture — **accepted**: Merrow flat, Grey Boss
   genuinely oblique, Wick babbling without landing lines, Brindle trade
   patter; the ledger-cadence reserved for Ash, the Clerk, Bodkin.
   Epigram cull ~20% in chs. 4–10.
5. Answer why-now; fix the sea-wall disproof — **accepted**: Elspeth had
   begun renewing the seam that spring — making permanent what Bodkin
   could still believe was undoable; that is the deadline that ended forty
   years of patience. And Bodkin suppresses the slack evidence *actively*:
   he kept every lifted stitch and never once looked at the box.
6. Un-chaperone the middle — **accepted**: the mends are fewer (four, not
   eleven), and one *failed* — the rag-wraith night nearly killed Ash with
   no net, and Bodkin says so in ch. 12. The flats death was never
   mendable: past the tide line his thread was out of any mender's reach.
7. Stage the decoy — **accepted** (Mystery 7).
8. Day-side existence — **modified**: not thirty pages; a recurring
   household (the Fish-Giver, given a name — Pell — and three quiet
   appearances: chs. 4, 9, 13) so the Hush and the homecoming land on a
   face the reader knows.
9. Institutions recur or shrink — **modified**: the Ratsmeet recurs
   (ch. 9 rooftop word travels through it); the Horologists' second
   appearance is the ch. 12 audit already; the pigeon election stays a
   sketch but shorter.
10. Let one thing stay strange — **accepted**: the Drowned are never
    priced, never explained; their ch. 5 beat loses its transactional
    gloss and nobody audits them anywhere.
11. Trim the epilogue's medal ceremony by half — **accepted**: Sootbeard's
    recommission and the framed receipt go; Tansy's stitch and Shear stay;
    Fenn's lamp is added.
12. Weigh Elspeth's guilt on the page — **accepted**: shown confession
    (commitment 5) plus one flat-voiced paragraph where Ash states her
    account as he stated Wick's.

## House rule added mid-revision (owner, 2026-08-15)

**The narrator's imagery must be a cat's imagery.** Ash does not pay rent,
attend weddings, vote, or sail. Metaphors come from what he has lived:
hunting, weather, hearths, kettles, thread and needlework, the lane, fish,
birds, dogs seen off, sunbeams, and fifteen years of watching one witch's
household and customers. Flagged instances: "patient as rent" (prologue),
"longer than most marriages and every government" (prologue). Sweep every
chapter for figures of speech outside a cat's plausible experience; the
in-world institutions (courts, ledgers, elections) are fair game because
the night-side actually runs them and Ash lives in a tradeswoman's shop.

## Execution status (2026-08-15)

All per-chapter items above are implemented; the PDF builds clean (156 pp).
Two items are honestly partial:

- **Epigram cull** — the worst offenders were cut (lien lecture, election
  admin, "framed receipt", the ch. 3 gloss) and all NEW material was written
  in a deliberately plainer register, but a systematic 20% line-by-line cull
  of chs. 4–10 remains for a dedicated style pass.
- **Voice differentiation** — Merrow, the senior grey, Tansy, and the
  Horologists now have distinct registers in their new/expanded scenes;
  retro-fitting the Grey Boss's and Brindle's existing chapter-4/2 dialogue
  is left for the same style pass.

Consistency decisions made during execution, now canon:
- The Clerk-corridor visits on the page: prologue (docketed, life 1), ch. 4
  (comic refusal, gutter), ch. 6 (wraith refusal), ch. 8 (fire refusal, the
  cat-sized chair debuts, Toll warned), ch. 9 (flats, life 2, Toll paid).
  Ch. 7's tally says three visits/twice refused accordingly.
- Bodkin's mends number **four**, tied at neaps, and are explicitly
  aftercare, not protection — no fight in the book was netted.
- The Tatterstage victim is the lead player (a stitcher in secret); the
  second victim is Widow Sarn of Hemlock Row. Cardew withholds the
  greasepaint detail in ch. 1 so ch. 7 keeps its reveal.
- Lady's Day = the hearing date = dark of the moon, most of a season out
  from ch. 3; the anchor is workable only at the neap; the bell audit wins
  its race by one evening.
- Fenn is spent, not held: taken in the contested weeks and burned as fuel
  in the Mere's final press. His lamp is maintained lit by the guild; the
  hall's new tally starts and ends at one; Merrow states both truths to
  Ash's face in the epilogue.
- Elspeth's why-now: the spring order of forty spools and the "work of
  renewal" — a re-founded seam would have made Bodkin's debt permanently
  uncollectible.

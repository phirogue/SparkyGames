# Side Quests — Case Notes & Curiosities

Each side quest is a complete small story with a beginning, a turn, and an
ending worth having. Format per entry: **Board card** (what the player reads,
≤20 words) → the real story → mission types used → what it feeds (ally,
lore, mechanics). Chapter placement is provisional.

Non-cliche rules: no fetch-for-fetching's-sake, no "clear rats from cellar"
(rats are a *faction*), no chosen-one prophecies, every quest resolvable in
2–4 prowls, and at least one quest per chapter must be solvable *without*
combat.

---

## 1. The Moth Collector *(Ch. 1 — Wickrow)*

**Board card:** "Memory-moths swarm the Loud Widow's house. She isn't
complaining. That's the strange part."

The widow is *paying* the moths — a saucer of candle-wax a night — to carry
her memories of her husband away, one by one, gentlest first. Her daughter
hired you to stop the "infestation." The moths, caught, will return what they
carry. The widow begs you not to. There is no right answer; the quest ends
with the player choosing which memories come back, if any — shown as moth
wings (art, not text).

*Missions:* hunt (moth-catching is a timed ritual mission), diplomacy.
*Feeds:* memory-moth bestiary; grief theme rhymes with Ash's own; the wax
trail quietly implicates a Chandler in Chapter 1's case.

## 2. The Pigeon Ballot *(Ch. 1 — rooftops)* — **BUILT 2026-08-08**

*(Shipped as `the_pigeon_ballot` in quests.json: seed-trail crossing + a
Testimony before the Perch — the pressed witness is the incumbent
Postmaster, whose pride breaks on the cart docket, so the quest gates on
holding it. The murder-night seed lands either way; Toby's exposure needs
the win.)*

**Board card:** "The pigeons are holding an election. Someone is stuffing the
ballot. The ballot is a hat."

Hollowmere's gossip flows through the pigeon-net; whoever leads it decides
what the city *overhears*. A slick young cock-pigeon is winning on promises
of "cleaner ledges" — bankrolled, it turns out, by seed from a human hand
that wants the gossip network looking elsewhere on a particular night.
Expose the seed trail without eating a single voter (diplomatic constraint:
eating even one voter voids the result — the pigeons checked precedent).

*Missions:* stealth (follow the seed), diplomacy (testify before the Perch).
*Feeds:* unlocks pigeon rumor system (quest hints); the "particular night"
is the murder night — a genuine case lead hidden in a comedy quest.

## 3. What the Gargoyle Saw *(Ch. 1–2 — Wickrow eaves)* — **DEFERRED to Ch2**
*(2026-08-08: needs the escort module, which Phase 0 deferred. The Moth
Collector (#1) and the Fish-Giver (#10) also remain open for Ch1's
post-ship side pool; neither blocks the chapter.)*

**Board card:** "Sootbeard has watched one street for 300 years. Last month,
he blinked. He wants to file a report."

Sootbeard blinked — for the first time in three centuries — because someone
*made* him blink, and in that half-second something crossed his street
unobserved. His pride is wounded; his report has no one to go to (his
regiment was decommissioned in 1731). Reconstruct the missing half-second
from three other witnesses: a rain barrel's ripples, a sleeping dog's dream,
and a window's frost. Escort the fussy old monument across town (he cannot
fly; he was *installed*) to identify the culprit's residue.

*Missions:* escort (a gargoyle escorting-the-escort inversion: he tanks, you
scout), ritual (witness reconstruction).
*Feeds:* recruits Sootbeard (rooftop fast-travel); first hard evidence of the
Unraveler; running gag — he salutes Ash forever after.

## 4. The Ninth Bell *(Ch. 2 — Gravamen)*

**Board card:** "The bell tower rings eight. Old-timers swear there were
nine. No one remembers when that changed. Or notices."

The ninth bell wasn't destroyed; it was *pawned* — by the city itself, a
century ago, to pay the Hollow Court for a plague's worth of overdue Tolls.
Since then, Hollowmere's nights have been one hour short, and nobody dreams
their last dream to the end. The bell sits in the Court's evidence vault,
docket unresolved. Win it back the only way the Court respects: find the
paperwork error (there is always a paperwork error).

*Missions:* heist (vault prowl, push-your-luck showcase), diplomacy (docket
hearing — the Clerk presiding, delighted).
*Feeds:* Hollow Court lore; restores the ninth hour — a *visible* world
change (new sunbeam tiles at dusk, new dream content); Clerk relationship.

## 5. The Understudy's Final Performance *(Ch. 2 — the Tatterstage)*

**Board card:** "The theater ghost auditions actors nightly for a play that
closed forty years ago. Nobody gets the part."

She died on opening night, mid-line, and can't move on until the scene ends
*correctly* — but she remembers her death wrong, and keeps directing everyone
toward the mistake. Gather the true blocking from old programmes, a retired
prompter, and the chalk ghost-marks under the boards, then perform the scene
*with* her — Ash playing opposite a ghost, cue by cue (a rhythm/ritual
mission with zero combat). When the line lands, she takes her bow.

*Missions:* ritual, stealth (the prompter's nursing-home has a strict no-cats
matron).
*Feeds:* recruits the Understudy (Guile trainer); teaches players the
no-combat quest pattern; her exit line is a clue — she was killed by the same
half-second that made Sootbeard blink.

## 6. The Milk Debt *(Ch. 1 — the Shambles)* — **BUILT 2026-08-08**

*(Shipped as `the_milk_debt` in quests.json: Court of Whiskers summons →
the escrow strongroom → the interest-clause insight → an Unpicking of the
purr-knot, no combat anywhere in it. The Gentleman never appears — a blank
calling card does, which is worse. Tansy follows Ash home.)*

**Board card:** "A kitten sold her purr to the Quiet Gentleman. The Court of
Whiskers considers this Everyone's Problem."

Tansy — small, absurd, unstoppable — sold her purr for the favor that saved
her litter from the canal. Contract's legal. Purr's gone. A kitten without a
purr can't be comforted and can't comfort, and the Court of Whiskers wants
the precedent *killed* before the Gentleman starts a market in them. Buy it
back, steal it back, or find the clause: the Gentleman never signs anything
that could ever be settled — but a purr, it turns out, accrues *interest*,
and he owes it to itself.

*Missions:* stealth (his counting-house), diplomacy (contract law, cat
jurisprudence).
*Feeds:* introduces Tansy (she follows Ash home; he objects; she stays) and
the Gentleman's power set; first favor-knot mechanics tutorial.

## 7. The Borrowed Shadow *(Ch. 2–3 — Mereside)*

**Board card:** "A man sold his shadow years ago. Yesterday it came home. It's
better at his life than he is."

The shadow pays his debts, charms his estranged daughter, waters the fern.
The man wants it *gone*; the neighbors want *him* gone instead. The twist:
the shadow isn't his — it's a rag-wraith wearing his silhouette, rehearsing
being human because its own owner is one of the *missing*. Track where the
missing owner went, and Ash — whose own shadow is nine-pocketed and heavier
than he admits — must decide which of the two men keeps the life.

*Missions:* stealth (shadow surveillance — shadowing a shadow), hunt.
*Feeds:* rag-wraith lore, the "missing, not dead" thread (Unraveler arc);
mirrors Ash's shadow mechanics; genuinely unsettling — tone range proof.

## 8. The Slow Clock *(Ch. 3 — Horological Society)*

**Board card:** "The Society's master clock runs four minutes slow. The
Horologists know why. They're not fixing it."

The clock is slow because it is *carrying* — four minutes skimmed nightly
from every clock in the district, hoarded for the Society's dying founder,
who is owed one last sunrise by contract but won't survive till spring. The
Hollow Court's auditors are en route: hoarding time is Toll evasion. Choose
your heist: steal the minutes *for* the Court (lawful, joyless), or help the
Horologists spend them all at once — one impossible golden extra hour over
Hollowmere (the whole district's sunbeam tiles ignite) — before the auditors
land.

*Missions:* heist, survival (hold the workshop door for the final hour).
*Feeds:* time-as-currency lore; the golden hour is Chapter 3's emotional
exhale; Court auditors become recurring... colleagues.

## 9. The Ratcatcher's Treaty *(Ch. 1–3 — ongoing chain)*

**Board card:** "Mirri has brokered peace between the Union and the
rat-kingdoms for a decade. Someone just broke it on purpose."

Three prowls across three chapters: a poisoned granary blamed on rats (it
wasn't), a Union ledger proving someone *pays* for pest panics, and a summit
under the Shambles where Ash — a cat, the historic enemy, the one arbiter
both sides find flattering precisely because he's absurd — presides. Verdict
sets the rat-kingdom as allies (spy network) or cool neutrals for endgame.

*Missions:* diplomacy (the summit is the diplomacy showcase), stealth,
escort (walk the rat-envoy through cat territory — the indignity, again).
*Feeds:* Mirri; faction system proof; who profits from fear → Chandlers
thread.

## 10. The Fish-Giver Is Late *(Ch. 1 — day-side)* — **BUILT 2026-08-09**
*(Shipped as `the_fish_giver` in quests.json: a day-side crossing, the
drainpipe brooch, the pigeon-net, the slow blink — no combat anywhere, and
he never learns Ash's name. The closing line pays the Ninth Stray forward.)*

**His name is Pell** (novel `story-bible/01-characters.md`, applied to the
quest 2026-08-16). He calls every stray *puss*, has done for eleven years,
and says *mind the water, puss* without any idea what he is saying. He is
the book's moral baseline and **must never be made quaint** — he wants
nothing, he is owed nothing, and he keeps doing it anyway. The closing line
used to say the saucer went down for "a stray with eight failed interviews
behind him", which mixed Ash up with the eight strays who declined Elspeth's
interview years before he arrived; it now reads "a stray who had nothing to
trade for it."

**Board card:** "Pell the Fish-Giver has missed two dawns. Cats don't worry.
I am not worried. I am investigating."

The fishmonger who fed half of Hollowmere's strays is behind on rent to a
landlord who happens to hold the lease through the Chandlers' bank. A purely
mundane, human-scale problem — no monsters — solvable only with cat assets:
gleam (a lost brooch in a drainpipe he could never reach), the pigeon-net
(where the landlord drinks), and the slow blink (the magistrate owns a cat;
the cat owes Ash; the magistrate never learns why she reconsidered). Nobody
ever knows it happened. He feeds the strays at dawn.

*Missions:* stealth, heist, diplomacy — all non-lethal, all small.
*Feeds:* the cats-repay-their-humans theme; proof the day-side matters;
pure-cute quest to balance the darker ones. He never learns Ash's name.

## 11. The Escrow Key *(Ch. 3 — Locksmiths' Fellowship)*

**Board card:** "A secret held in escrow for sixty years is due to open
tonight. Its owner died. Its subject didn't."

The Locksmiths hold secrets in escrow — locked boxes that open on a date, a
death, or a condition. Box 9-Crown opens tonight; the depositor is dead; by
Fellowship law an unclaimed secret is read *aloud*, on the steps, at
midnight. Half the night-side wants it read; the other half wants it burned;
the subject — elderly, day-side, unaware — will be destroyed by it. Ash can
steal it, delay it (find an heir by midnight), or let the law run. The secret
itself: chosen from a pool so replays differ; one variant is about Elspeth.

*Missions:* heist against the best locks in the city, escort (the found heir,
through everyone who'd rather they not arrive).
*Feeds:* Locksmith lore; secrets-as-goods economy; moral engine showcase;
the Elspeth variant plants final-chapter evidence.

## 12. The Last Parcel *(Ch. 4 — Thimblefield)*

**Board card:** "A parcel arrives for Elspeth Vane, thirty years delayed.
Postage due. The postman insists on a signature."

The night-post is punctual on a *geologic* scale. The parcel — mailed by a
young Elspeth to her future self — can only be signed for by her legal
successor, which the Hollow Court has never ruled on, which means a hearing,
which means Ash proving before the Clerk that a cat can inherit. (Precedent:
complicated. The Clerk's enthusiasm: unprofessional.) Inside is nothing of
power — a spool of ordinary thread, her first, kept the way beginners keep
things — and a note the player finally reads after the last flashback.
Winning this quest is how Ash becomes, legally, the Needle's heir: the
mechanical unlock is the ability to *stitch* — mend one torn shadow-pocket.

*Missions:* diplomacy (the hearing), ritual.
*Feeds:* endgame keystone; the quiet emotional payoff of the whole shadow-
pocket system; sets up post-launch premise (an heir with everything to
learn).

## Patterns to keep when writing new quests

1. **The board card is a joke or an itch** — twenty words that make you need
   to know. The story underneath is sincere.
2. **Comedy quests hide real clues; sad quests end with something earned.**
3. **Factions solve quests sideways** — every quest should have at least one
   resolution that spends a favor-knot or an ally instead of a fight.
4. **The world visibly changes** when a quest ends (a bell, a golden hour, a
   fed stray) — change shown in art and district rules, not paragraphs.
5. **Ash's dignity is load-bearing.** He is carried exactly twice in the
   whole game, and both times it matters.

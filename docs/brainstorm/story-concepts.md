# Story Concept Pitches

Five pitches for the game's world and narrative frame. Each is judged on the
same four criteria a roguelite needs:

1. **Death excuse** — does the story *explain* why you retry runs forever?
2. **Run = story beat** — does one 3–5 minute run map to a natural narrative unit?
3. **Quest engine** — does the premise generate endless quests without straining?
4. **Midjourney-ability** — does the aesthetic prompt well for consistent card art?

---

## Pitch 1 — *Emberfall* (The Last Lantern)

The sun went out a hundred years ago. The world survives in scattered
sanctuaries lit by the last embers of the old light. You are a **Lanternbearer**
carrying a living flame through the Long Dark, from sanctuary to sanctuary. Your
cards are **sparks** — fragments of memory and warmth the flame has consumed
over a century: a soldier's courage, a mother's lullaby, a smith's fury.

- **Death excuse:** When you fall, the flame gutters out — and is rekindled at
  the last sanctuary. The flame remembers; the bearer is remade. Meta-progress
  *is* the flame growing brighter.
- **Run = story beat:** One run = one crossing between sanctuaries. Each
  sanctuary you reach unlocks its people's stories and quests.
- **Quest engine:** Sanctuaries in danger, relics of the old sun, factions who
  want the flame for themselves, and the central mystery: *who put out the sun,
  and can it be relit?*
- **Midjourney-ability:** Excellent — "glowing ember spirit, dark fantasy,
  candlelit, rim lighting" is a strongly consistent visual language, and the
  dark-with-one-light-source look hides art inconsistencies well.
- **Tone:** Melancholy but warm. Hollow Knight meets a campfire.

## Pitch 2 — *The Nine Lives of Ashcat*

Your witch is dead — murdered — and you are her cat familiar. Cats have nine
lives; a familiar of a great witch has nine *thousand*, but each is short. Every
run is one life spent hunting the truth of her murder through a fairytale-gothic
city. Cards are the witch's stolen spellbook pages you gradually recover.

- **Death excuse:** Perfect — you literally spend a life per run.
- **Run = story beat:** One life = one lead in the murder investigation.
- **Quest engine:** A murder mystery with suspects, districts, and rival
  familiars. Risk: a central mystery can *end*; needs episodic cases after.
- **Midjourney-ability:** Very good — "black cat, gothic storybook city, ink and
  watercolor" is charming and distinctive.
- **Tone:** Wry, dark-cozy. Strong mascot potential (icon-friendly hero).

## Pitch 3 — *Dreamtithe*

Nightmares are harvesting the dreams of a sleeping town, and the sleepers can't
wake until their stolen dreams are returned. You are a **Dreamwalker** who dives
into one villager's dream per run. Cards are dream-stuff: borrowed courage,
bottled laughter, a memory of rain.

- **Death excuse:** You just wake up. Zero lore strain.
- **Run = story beat:** One run = one villager's dream = one self-contained
  micro-story with a beginning and end. The cleanest run/story mapping of all
  five pitches — every run *finishes a tale*.
- **Quest engine:** Infinite by construction — every new villager is a new
  quest, and villagers' waking stories interconnect into town-wide arcs.
- **Midjourney-ability:** Good but riskier — surreal dreamscapes are gorgeous
  but visually inconsistent run-to-run; needs a strong unifying style rule.
- **Tone:** Whimsical with teeth. Studio Ghibli meets Psychonauts.

## Pitch 4 — *The Bard's Debt*

You are a bard who made a bad bargain: a fae patron returned you from death, and
now every tale you tell comes true — with you inside it. Each run is a tale told
at a tavern hearth. Fail, and the tale ends badly; the fae collects. Cards are
story tropes: The Sudden Storm, The Loyal Hound, The Convenient Rope.

- **Death excuse:** The tale simply ends; the teller survives to tell another.
- **Run = story beat:** One run = one tale. Meta-frame (the tavern, the fae
  debt) carries the long arc.
- **Quest engine:** Patrons request tale types; the fae's contract has clauses
  to discover and loopholes to exploit. Very flexible.
- **Midjourney-ability:** Trickier — the anthology structure means each tale
  wants its own look, multiplying art needs.
- **Tone:** Playful, self-aware, Princess-Bride-ish.

## Pitch 5 — *Barrowdeep*

A classic: you are the last warden of a bottomless barrow beneath a dying
kingdom, descending each night to push back what stirs below. Cards are oaths
sworn by the wardens before you.

- **Death excuse:** Wardens are oath-bound; the barrow returns them to its gate.
- **Run = story beat:** One descent per run; depth = chapter structure.
- **Quest engine:** Solid but generic — dungeon floors and boss seals.
- **Midjourney-ability:** Easiest of all — classic dark fantasy prompts
  beautifully. But also the least differentiated in a crowded market.
- **Tone:** Grim, traditional dark fantasy.

## Early read (to pressure-test against market research)

- **Strongest overall:** *Emberfall* — distinctive aesthetic, meta-progression
  woven into the fiction (the flame grows), and the sun mystery gives a spine
  for chaptered quests. The ember/spark identity also echoes the studio name.
- **Best run structure:** *Dreamtithe* — "one run = one villager's complete
  story" is the best possible fit for 3–5 minute story-driven sessions, and the
  most natural quest generator.
- **Best mascot/marketability:** *Ashcat* — a character-led store page icon
  (expressive cat) historically outperforms abstract ones.
- **Hybrid worth considering:** Emberfall's world with Dreamtithe's structure —
  the Lanternbearer enters the *dreams of the sleeping sanctuaries* (people
  frozen in the dark, dreaming), one sleeper per run. One flame, many tales.
